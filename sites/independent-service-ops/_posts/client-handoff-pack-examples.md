Examples make freelancer client project handoff and offboarding checklists easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases freelancers and independent professional service businesses can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A website launches but analytics ownership remains on the freelancer's account

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Client and project, Deliverable, Final file or system link, then move it through inventory deliverables and ownership and prepare files and secure access transfer. If a deliverable lacks an agreed final format or owner, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Final design files are present but fonts and licenses are undocumented

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Deliverable, Final file or system link, Owner after handoff, then move it through inventory deliverables and ownership and prepare files and secure access transfer. If access transfer is incomplete or insecure, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: The client accepts delivery while one content item has a named post-launch date

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Final file or system link, Owner after handoff, Access transfer status, then move it through inventory deliverables and ownership and prepare files and secure access transfer. If the client identifies an unresolved item during review, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make the handoff has a human-readable index?
- Did the record make access transfer follows appropriate security practice?
- Did the record make acceptance criteria come from the agreed scope?
- Did the record make open items have owners, dates, and support boundaries?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
