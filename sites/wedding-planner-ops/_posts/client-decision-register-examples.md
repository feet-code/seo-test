Examples make wedding client decision tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent wedding planners and boutique planning teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: The couple must choose a floor-plan option before rentals are reserved

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Wedding and decision ID, Question and decision category, Options and recommendation, then move it through open the decision with context and options and set the decision owner and needed-by date. If a needed-by date approaches without an approved answer, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A text changes the cake design after an email approval

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Question and decision category, Options and recommendation, Needed-by date and consequence, then move it through open the decision with context and options and set the decision owner and needed-by date. If two channels contain conflicting client choices, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A rain-plan decision changes transportation and tent requirements

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Options and recommendation, Needed-by date and consequence, Decision makers, then move it through open the decision with context and options and set the decision owner and needed-by date. If a vendor constraint changes the available options, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open client decision needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every decision that blocks budget, design, or vendor work has one approved answer, effective version, and downstream owner?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep approved wedding plan, contract, and project workspace as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
