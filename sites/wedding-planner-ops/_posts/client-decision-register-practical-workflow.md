Important choices are discussed in meetings, texts, and mood boards, making it difficult to know what the couple approved and which vendor work can proceed. For independent wedding planners and boutique planning teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every decision that blocks budget, design, or vendor work has one approved answer, effective version, and downstream owner**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open client decision needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every decision that blocks budget, design, or vendor work has one approved answer, effective version, and downstream owner
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep approved wedding plan, contract, and project workspace as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Open the decision with context and options

Record **Wedding and decision ID** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can set the decision owner and needed-by date, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Set the decision owner and needed-by date

Record **Question and decision category** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can collect the couple's approved answer, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Collect the couple's approved answer

Record **Options and recommendation** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can record consequences and affected deliverables, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Record consequences and affected deliverables

Record **Needed-by date and consequence** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can publish the decision and close superseded versions, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Publish the decision and close superseded versions

Record **Decision makers** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a needed-by date approaches without an approved answer
- two channels contain conflicting client choices
- a vendor constraint changes the available options

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
