A checklist for wedding client decision tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent wedding planners and boutique planning teams and centers on one result: **every decision that blocks budget, design, or vendor work has one approved answer, effective version, and downstream owner**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Wedding and decision ID
- Confirm Question and decision category
- Confirm Options and recommendation
- Confirm Needed-by date and consequence

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the decision with context and options
- Update Set the decision owner and needed-by date
- Update Collect the couple's approved answer
- Update Record consequences and affected deliverables
- Update Publish the decision and close superseded versions

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Decision makers
- Verify Approved answer and evidence
- Verify Effective version
- Verify Affected vendors and follow-up actions

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a needed-by date approaches without an approved answer
- [ ] Review records where two channels contain conflicting client choices
- [ ] Review records where a vendor constraint changes the available options

- [ ] Check for treating a meeting discussion as final approval
- [ ] Check for letting two contradictory answers remain active
- [ ] Check for recording the choice without its budget or timeline effect
- [ ] Check for not notifying vendors when a decision is revised

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Decision lead time, Late decision count, Revision impact rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
