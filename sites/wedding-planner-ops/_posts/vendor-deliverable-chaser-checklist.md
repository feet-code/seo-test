A checklist for wedding vendor deliverable tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent wedding planners and boutique planning teams and centers on one result: **every contracted vendor deliverable is received, reviewed, and reflected in the current wedding plan before its dependency date**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Wedding and vendor
- Confirm Contract requirement
- Confirm Deliverable description
- Confirm Due date and dependency date

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Extract the deliverable and deadline from the contract
- Update Assign the vendor contact and internal reviewer
- Update Request the required file or confirmation
- Update Review and resolve missing or conflicting details
- Update Approve the deliverable and update dependent plans

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Vendor contact and planner owner
- Verify Request and reminder history
- Verify Review status and issue
- Verify Approved version and downstream update

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a deliverable is not received by its reminder threshold
- [ ] Review records where a submitted file conflicts with the contract or current plan
- [ ] Review records where a wedding decision changes a previously approved vendor requirement

- [ ] Check for tracking a vendor invoice but not the operational deliverable
- [ ] Check for accepting an attachment without recording its version
- [ ] Check for sending reminders after a later email already supplied the answer
- [ ] Check for changing the master plan without preserving the approved source

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time deliverable rate, Review turnaround, Late dependency exposure. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
