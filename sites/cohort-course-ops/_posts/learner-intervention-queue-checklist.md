A checklist for cohort course learner engagement and intervention tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent cohort-course creators and small training businesses and centers on one result: **learners who may need support receive timely, respectful outreach tied to a concrete next step**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Cohort and learner
- Confirm Signal and source
- Confirm Signal date
- Confirm Context known
- Confirm Outreach owner

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Define transparent engagement signals
- Update Review the learner context
- Update Assign personal outreach
- Update Agree on a support next step
- Update Record the outcome and next review

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Contact channel
- Verify Learner response
- Verify Support next step
- Verify Review date
- Verify Outcome

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a defined combination of attendance, assignment, or communication signals appears
- [ ] Review records where the learner requests help or a schedule change
- [ ] Review records where outreach receives no response by the agreed review date

- [ ] Check for treating one missed event as proof of disengagement
- [ ] Check for sending automated pressure without checking context
- [ ] Check for collecting sensitive learner information that is not needed
- [ ] Check for closing the record because a message was sent

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Reviewed-signal time, Support-plan acceptance, Resolved intervention mix. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
