A checklist for portable restroom delivery placement readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for portable restroom rental and recurring sanitation service operators and centers on one result: **every delivery is released with the correct units, approved placement evidence, safe access, onsite contact, and recurring-service clearance**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer site order and event
- Confirm Unit types quantities and identifiers
- Confirm Requested placement and map
- Confirm Approver and onsite contact

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Confirm order unit mix and dates
- Update Collect site map and placement approval
- Update Review truck access surface and service path
- Update Resolve site or inventory exceptions
- Update Release delivery and verify placed units

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Surface slope overhead and access conditions
- Verify Service truck clearance and frequency
- Verify Delivery window pickup date and restrictions
- Verify Placed photo coordinates and driver confirmation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a delivery or relocation is scheduled
- [ ] Review records where placement access or unit mix remains unconfirmed
- [ ] Review records where the driver cannot use the approved placement

- [ ] Check for accepting a pin with no placement approver
- [ ] Check for planning delivery access but not weekly service access
- [ ] Check for sending an unverified unit type
- [ ] Check for leaving units wherever the driver can fit them without documenting change

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are First-attempt placement rate, Placement decision time, Early relocation rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
