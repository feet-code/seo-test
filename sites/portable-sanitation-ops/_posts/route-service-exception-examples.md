Examples make portable restroom route service exception tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases portable restroom rental and recurring sanitation service operators can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A locked gate blocks two of six units

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer site and route stop, Unit identifiers and expected count, Service time driver and GPS, then move it through capture the exception by unit and stop and record completed versus blocked service. If a driver cannot complete normal unit service, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: One unit was moved behind stored materials

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Unit identifiers and expected count, Service time driver and GPS, Completed service and supply quantities, then move it through capture the exception by unit and stop and record completed versus blocked service. If damage overuse or relocation changes contract treatment, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: An event unit needs an emergency extra service

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Service time driver and GPS, Completed service and supply quantities, Exception cause photos and condition, then move it through capture the exception by unit and stop and record completed versus blocked service. If a recovery visit fails or becomes overdue, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open unit service exception needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every incomplete or abnormal unit service has unit-level evidence, customer impact, owner, billing treatment, and a verified recovery outcome?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the portable-sanitation customer, contract, unit, delivery, route, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
