A checklist for portable restroom route service exception tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for portable restroom rental and recurring sanitation service operators and centers on one result: **every incomplete or abnormal unit service has unit-level evidence, customer impact, owner, billing treatment, and a verified recovery outcome**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer site and route stop
- Confirm Unit identifiers and expected count
- Confirm Service time driver and GPS
- Confirm Completed service and supply quantities

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the exception by unit and stop
- Update Record completed versus blocked service
- Update Classify cause impact and urgency
- Update Notify the customer and schedule response
- Update Verify recovery and update unit history

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Exception cause photos and condition
- Verify Customer notice and contract treatment
- Verify Recovery action owner and due time
- Verify Verified outcome and next-route note

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a driver cannot complete normal unit service
- [ ] Review records where damage overuse or relocation changes contract treatment
- [ ] Review records where a recovery visit fails or becomes overdue

- [ ] Check for recording only the site when one unit is affected
- [ ] Check for marking all units serviced after partial access
- [ ] Check for charging an exception without usable evidence
- [ ] Check for scheduling recovery with the same blocked instructions

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Unit service completion, Recovery cycle time, Repeat exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
