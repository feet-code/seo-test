A checklist for catering event change control should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent caterers and small event-food teams and centers on one result: **every accepted event change has authority, cost and production impact, an effective version, and acknowledgment from affected owners**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Event and current version
- Confirm Requested change and source
- Confirm Request time and applicable cutoff
- Confirm Cost and contract impact

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Log the requested change against the current event order
- Update Assess cutoff, cost, supply, and staffing impact
- Update Obtain client and internal approval
- Update Publish the new effective event version
- Update Collect acknowledgments and close superseded instructions

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Production, rental, and staffing impact
- Verify Client and internal approvals
- Verify Effective version and distribution
- Verify Affected-owner acknowledgment

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a requested change crosses a contractual or production cutoff
- [ ] Review records where the change affects cost, safety, staffing, rentals, or another vendor
- [ ] Review records where an affected owner has not acknowledged the effective version

- [ ] Check for editing the event order without preserving the request
- [ ] Check for accepting a late change before checking production feasibility
- [ ] Check for sending a revised pdf without identifying what changed
- [ ] Check for letting an email override the effective kitchen version

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Late-change rate, Approval turnaround, Acknowledgment completeness. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
