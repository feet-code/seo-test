Dietary requests arrive through proposals, guest lists, planners, and last-minute emails, while kitchens need one approved interpretation tied to menu and service decisions. For independent caterers and small event-food teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every declared dietary or allergen requirement is clarified, approved into the event plan, and communicated to production and service owners**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open dietary requirement needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every declared dietary or allergen requirement is clarified, approved into the event plan, and communicated to production and service owners
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep signed event order, recipe, allergen, and production systems as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Capture the request and original wording

Record **Event and guest identifier** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can clarify guest, severity context, and contact path, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Clarify guest, severity context, and contact path

Record **Original request and source** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can review menu feasibility with authorized staff, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Review menu feasibility with authorized staff

Record **Dietary or allergen category** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can approve preparation and service controls, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Approve preparation and service controls

Record **Clarification status and contact** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can publish the final register and confirm late exceptions, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Publish the final register and confirm late exceptions

Record **Affected menu items** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a request is ambiguous or missing a guest count
- the requested accommodation conflicts with menu or facility controls
- a new requirement arrives after the production cutoff

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
