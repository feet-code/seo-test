A checklist for pet boarding pickup readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent pet boarding facilities and dog daycare operators and centers on one result: **every departing pet is released to an authorized person with belongings, balance, and approved stay handoff reconciled**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Pet, owner, and stay
- Confirm Expected pickup window
- Confirm Pet and housing location
- Confirm Belongings inventory

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Flag the stay for expected pickup
- Update Reconcile pet location, services, and belongings
- Update Prepare the approved owner-facing handoff
- Update Verify collector authority and payment
- Update Record release and any remaining follow-up

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Completed add-on services
- Verify Approved stay-note summary
- Verify Balance and authorized collector
- Verify Release time, recipient, and exception

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a pickup window approaches
- [ ] Review records where the collector, time, service, or balance changes
- [ ] Review records where a belonging, stay note, or pet location is unresolved

- [ ] Check for preparing release before confirming the pet's current location
- [ ] Check for sharing internal staff notes as owner-facing guidance
- [ ] Check for releasing to a person not listed or verified
- [ ] Check for closing the stay while belongings or charges remain unresolved

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Pickup preparation lead, Pickup exception rate, Release dwell time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
