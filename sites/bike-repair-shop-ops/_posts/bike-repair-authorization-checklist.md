A checklist for bike repair estimate approval tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent bicycle repair shops and service departments and centers on one result: **every material repair change has an itemized current estimate, recorded customer decision, parts implication, and explicit mechanic release**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer bicycle and work order
- Confirm Intake complaint and authorized ceiling
- Confirm Inspection findings and photos
- Confirm Labor parts and option lines

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Inspect and compare findings with intake scope
- Update Build the revised labor and parts options
- Update Send the estimate with a clear decision request
- Update Record approval decline or question
- Update Release only approved work and preserve the estimate version

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Safety impact and declined-work note
- Verify Estimate version price and validity
- Verify Customer response channel and time
- Verify Mechanic release parts action and due date

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where inspection finds work beyond the intake scope
- [ ] Review records where the customer changes budget or parts preference
- [ ] Review records where parts availability or diagnosis changes the estimate

- [ ] Check for performing additional work from a vague go ahead
- [ ] Check for replacing the original estimate instead of versioning
- [ ] Check for ordering special parts before decision
- [ ] Check for treating no response as approval for safety work

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Finding-to-decision time, Pre-work authorization rate, Estimate revision rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
