A checklist for bike repair pickup readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent bicycle repair shops and service departments and centers on one result: **every completed bicycle is quality-checked, fully assembled with customer property, reconciled financially, and staged before pickup notification**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer bicycle and work order
- Confirm Approved and completed work
- Confirm Torque safety and function checks
- Confirm Test ride or no-ride reason

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Confirm approved work and parts are complete
- Update Perform final safety and function checks
- Update Gather accessories keys batteries and saved parts
- Update Reconcile invoice balance and declined work
- Update Stage notify and record release to the customer

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Accessories keys battery and removed parts
- Verify Declined recommendations and explanation
- Verify Invoice deposit and balance
- Verify Staging location notification and release

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a mechanic marks approved work complete
- [ ] Review records where final review finds an unresolved item
- [ ] Review records where the customer arrives or requests third-party pickup

- [ ] Check for notifying when the mechanic says done
- [ ] Check for skipping a check because the repair was minor
- [ ] Check for separating a battery or key from the bicycle record
- [ ] Check for closing the work order while the balance or declined-work note is unclear

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-on-first-notice rate, Completion-to-notice time, Pickup exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
