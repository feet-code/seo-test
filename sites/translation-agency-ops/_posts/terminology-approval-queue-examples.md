Examples make translation terminology approval workflow easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases boutique translation agencies and localization project teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A product feature name has no approved German equivalent

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Client, project, and language pair, Source term and context, Screenshot or segment reference, then move it through open the source term with context and propose target terms and rationale. If a term blocks active translation near its needed-by time, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Two client reviewers choose different French terms

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Source term and context, Screenshot or segment reference, Proposed target terms, then move it through open the source term with context and propose target terms and rationale. If reviewers provide conflicting answers, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A legal term changes after three files are translated

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Screenshot or segment reference, Proposed target terms, Owner and authorized approver, then move it through open the source term with context and propose target terms and rationale. If an approved term changes after work has already used it, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open terminology decision needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every terminology question receives an authoritative decision that is applied to the glossary and affected translation work?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep tms, translation memory, glossary, and approved source files as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
