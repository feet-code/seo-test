A checklist for translation terminology approval workflow should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for boutique translation agencies and localization project teams and centers on one result: **every terminology question receives an authoritative decision that is applied to the glossary and affected translation work**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client, project, and language pair
- Confirm Source term and context
- Confirm Screenshot or segment reference
- Confirm Proposed target terms

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the source term with context
- Update Propose target terms and rationale
- Update Route to the authorized reviewer
- Update Record the approved or rejected decision
- Update Update language assets and notify affected work

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Owner and authorized approver
- Verify Needed-by date and work impact
- Verify Approved decision and rationale
- Verify Glossary version and affected-job update

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a term blocks active translation near its needed-by time
- [ ] Review records where reviewers provide conflicting answers
- [ ] Review records where an approved term changes after work has already used it

- [ ] Check for approving a term without source context
- [ ] Check for letting different reviewers approve conflicting translations
- [ ] Check for closing the question before updating the glossary
- [ ] Check for applying a project-specific choice across all clients

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Decision turnaround, Blocked-segment exposure, Terminology recurrence. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
