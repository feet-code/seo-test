A checklist for contractor job photo documentation and field office handoff should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for owner-operated HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and repair contractors and centers on one result: **the office receives a job-linked, labeled photo record that is sufficient for the next billing, customer, or service decision**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer and job number
- Confirm Technician
- Confirm Photo stage
- Confirm Equipment or area

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Define the required photo set
- Update Capture photos at the correct job stage
- Update Label context and exceptions
- Update Submit the field closeout
- Update Review and release the office workflow

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Caption
- Verify Timestamp
- Verify Exception
- Verify Customer-facing permission
- Verify Office review status

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a required stage photo is missing
- [ ] Review records where the technician identifies changed scope or concealed conditions
- [ ] Review records where an image may contain customer-sensitive information

- [ ] Check for uploading photos with no job or area label
- [ ] Check for taking only completion photos when before evidence matters
- [ ] Check for mixing private/internal and customer-facing images
- [ ] Check for closing the job before the office confirms the required set

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Complete photo-set rate, Office clarification rate, Handoff review time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
