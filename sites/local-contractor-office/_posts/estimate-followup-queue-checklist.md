A checklist for contractor estimate follow-up and quote tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for owner-operated HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and repair contractors and centers on one result: **every sent estimate reaches a documented customer decision or a deliberate next review date**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer and job
- Confirm Estimate number
- Confirm Sent date
- Confirm Delivery confirmation
- Confirm Estimate value band

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Confirm estimate delivery
- Update Schedule the first contextual follow-up
- Update Capture questions and changes
- Update Ask for the decision
- Update Close won, lost, deferred, or unreachable

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Next-contact date
- Verify Customer question
- Verify Revision status
- Verify Decision
- Verify Closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where delivery is unconfirmed after the send event
- [ ] Review records where the customer asks a scope, scheduling, or financing question
- [ ] Review records where the next-contact date passes without a logged outcome

- [ ] Check for sending did you see this with no job context
- [ ] Check for continuing reminders after the customer declines
- [ ] Check for treating no response as a price objection
- [ ] Check for failing to connect revised estimates to the original follow-up history

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Decision rate, Time to decision, Loss reason completeness. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
