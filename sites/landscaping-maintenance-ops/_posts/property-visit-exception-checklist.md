A checklist for landscape maintenance visit exception tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small commercial landscaping and recurring property-maintenance companies and centers on one result: **every incomplete landscape visit has quantified skipped work, evidence, contract treatment, customer communication, and a recovery decision**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and contract
- Confirm Visit date crew and route
- Confirm Planned service scope
- Confirm Completed and skipped tasks

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Record the exception at the property
- Update Separate completed from blocked scope
- Update Apply contract and operations rules
- Update Notify the customer and schedule recovery
- Update Verify recovered work or approved closure

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Exception cause and evidence
- Verify Contract billing or credit treatment
- Verify Customer notice and recovery owner
- Verify Recovery completion or closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a crew cannot complete planned scope
- [ ] Review records where an exception changes billing or customer expectation
- [ ] Review records where the promised recovery window becomes at risk

- [ ] Check for marking the whole visit complete after partial work
- [ ] Check for using weather as a reason without affected tasks
- [ ] Check for promising a return with no route capacity
- [ ] Check for crediting work before checking contract treatment

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Complete-visit rate, Recovery lead time, Repeat property exception. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
