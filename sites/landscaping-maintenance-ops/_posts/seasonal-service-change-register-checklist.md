A checklist for seasonal landscape service change tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small commercial landscaping and recurring property-maintenance companies and centers on one result: **every seasonal contract change has an effective date, customer approval, route impact, crew acknowledgment, and verified first execution**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and contract
- Confirm Current service plan and version
- Confirm Changed task frequency and season
- Confirm Effective and end dates

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the seasonal change from contract review
- Update Define old and new service rules
- Update Assess route labor material and price impact
- Update Obtain customer and operations approval
- Update Publish and verify the first affected visit

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Route labor and material impact
- Verify Price credit or billing change
- Verify Customer and operations approval
- Verify Crew acknowledgment and first-visit check

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a contract reaches a seasonal transition
- [ ] Review records where the customer requests a temporary frequency change
- [ ] Review records where the first affected visit conflicts with the published plan

- [ ] Check for editing the live plan without preserving the prior version
- [ ] Check for changing frequency without route capacity review
- [ ] Check for assuming silence means approval
- [ ] Check for publishing the change after the first affected visit

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time plan publication, First-visit accuracy, Unpriced change rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
