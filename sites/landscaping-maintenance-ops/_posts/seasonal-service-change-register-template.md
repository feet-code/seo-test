The most useful seasonal landscape service change tracking template is a small operating record. It should answer what is happening, who owns it, what evidence exists, and when the next decision occurs. This structure works in a spreadsheet, database, or focused application.

## Recommended record fields

| Field | Why it exists | Update point |
|---|---|---|
| Customer property and contract | Prevents the record from depending on memory or an inbox search | Open the seasonal change from contract review |
| Current service plan and version | Prevents the record from depending on memory or an inbox search | Define old and new service rules |

## Suggested statuses

Use workflow statuses that describe reality: **Open The Seasonal Change From Contract Review → Define Old And New Service Rules → Assess Route Labor Material And Price Impact → Obtain Customer And Operations Approval → Publish And Verify The First Affected Visit**. Add **Waiting** only when you also capture a waiting reason and review date. Add **Closed—Not Completed** when an item legitimately ends without the desired outcome.

## Follow-up rules

- When a contract reaches a seasonal transition, assign a next action and review date.
- When the customer requests a temporary frequency change, assign a next action and review date.
- When the first affected visit conflicts with the published plan, assign a next action and review date.

Avoid reminders with no stop condition. A rule should say when it starts, who receives it, what counts as a response, and when a person should take over.

## Example records

- Weekly mowing shifts to leaf cleanup tasks
- Irrigation checks pause after winterization
- A retail property increases service before holiday traffic

For each example, write the current status, next action, owner, and supporting evidence. This makes the template testable with real work rather than idealized sample data.

## Quality-control rules

- Every open contract service change needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every seasonal contract change has an effective date, customer approval, route impact, crew acknowledgment, and verified first execution
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep the landscape CRM, contract, estimate, route, crew, job-cost, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

Before adding automation, run the template manually for a week. Remove ambiguous fields and confirm that two different users classify the same situation the same way. Consistency matters more than having a long form.
