A checklist for landscape enhancement proposal approval tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small commercial landscaping and recurring property-maintenance companies and centers on one result: **every qualified enhancement request reaches a priced customer decision with field context, scope assumptions, and a scheduled or closed next action**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and contract
- Confirm Request source and desired outcome
- Confirm Location photos and measurements
- Confirm Existing condition and constraints

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the opportunity from field or customer
- Update Qualify site condition and desired result
- Update Create scope options and constraints
- Update Send and follow the proposal
- Update Schedule approved work or record the declined reason

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Scope option materials and labor
- Verify Price validity and exclusions
- Verify Customer decision and evidence
- Verify Scheduled window or closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a crew records work outside recurring scope
- [ ] Review records where the customer requests a property improvement
- [ ] Review records where conditions or price assumptions change before approval

- [ ] Check for submitting a photo with no desired result
- [ ] Check for building an estimate without site access constraints
- [ ] Check for letting a crew verbally quote unapproved work
- [ ] Check for following up after the proposal has materially expired

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Observation-to-proposal time, Proposal decision rate, Approved-to-scheduled time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
