Rain, locked areas, parked vehicles, irrigation leaks, debris, special events, and crew shortages alter recurring visits, but the contract consequence and recovery work can disappear in route notes. For small commercial landscaping and recurring property-maintenance companies, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every incomplete landscape visit has quantified skipped work, evidence, contract treatment, customer communication, and a recovery decision**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open visit exception needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every incomplete landscape visit has quantified skipped work, evidence, contract treatment, customer communication, and a recovery decision
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep the landscape CRM, contract, estimate, route, crew, job-cost, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Record the exception at the property

Record **Customer property and contract** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can separate completed from blocked scope, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Separate completed from blocked scope

Record **Visit date crew and route** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can apply contract and operations rules, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Apply contract and operations rules

Record **Planned service scope** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can notify the customer and schedule recovery, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Notify the customer and schedule recovery

Record **Completed and skipped tasks** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can verify recovered work or approved closure, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Verify recovered work or approved closure

Record **Exception cause and evidence** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a crew cannot complete planned scope
- an exception changes billing or customer expectation
- the promised recovery window becomes at risk

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
