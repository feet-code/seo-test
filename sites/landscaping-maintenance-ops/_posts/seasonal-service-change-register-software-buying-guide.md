Software for seasonal landscape service change tracking should be evaluated against the operating problem, not a generic feature checklist. For small commercial landscaping and recurring property-maintenance companies, a useful trial must demonstrate this outcome: **every seasonal contract change has an effective date, customer approval, route impact, crew acknowledgment, and verified first execution**.

## Write requirements from the workflow

The tool must support these steps without hidden spreadsheets: Open the seasonal change from contract review, Define old and new service rules, Assess route labor material and price impact, Obtain customer and operations approval, Publish and verify the first affected visit. It must also make these fields easy to capture at the moment work happens: Customer property and contract, Current service plan and version, Changed task frequency and season, Effective and end dates, Route labor and material impact, Price credit or billing change, Customer and operations approval, Crew acknowledgment and first-visit check.

## Use a live demo script

Ask the vendor—or your internal prototype—to complete these tasks:

- Create and resolve this test case: Weekly mowing shifts to leaf cleanup tasks
- Create and resolve this test case: Irrigation checks pause after winterization
- Create and resolve this test case: A retail property increases service before holiday traffic

Then test one waiting case, one reassignment, one closed-without-completion case, and one export. Do not accept a slide deck in place of the workflow.

## Score the trial

| Metric | Simple calculation | Decision it supports |
|---|---|---|
| On-time plan publication | changes published before effective date / approved changes | schedule seasonal reviews |
| First-visit accuracy | first visits matching new plan / first affected visits | check crew handoff |
| Unpriced change rate | changes executed without billing decision / changes executed | protect margin |

Add setup time, recurring administration, export quality, permission clarity, and mobile usability where relevant. Weight the score by frequency: a daily two-minute annoyance matters more than a rare advanced feature.

## Red flags

- Editing the live plan without preserving the prior version
- Changing frequency without route capacity review
- Assuming silence means approval
- Publishing the change after the first affected visit

Also be cautious when the product requires broad process migration before it can solve the narrow problem, or when basic history/export controls are unavailable.

## Make the decision with real records

Run a small trial using current work, not sanitized sample data. Compare the realistic alternatives below and record why the winning approach fits now:

| Approach | Best when | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Crew route sheets, property notes, photos, estimator emails, and manager texts | One owner handles low volume and can see every open item | Status and follow-up history depend on memory and inbox searches |
| Landscape-management software or a shared property-service board | The team already maintains it and exceptions are simple | Purpose-built reminders, evidence, and stop conditions require manual setup |
| A focused workflow tool | The same coordination failure repeats across many live records | It must integrate with the system of record and justify another workflow |
