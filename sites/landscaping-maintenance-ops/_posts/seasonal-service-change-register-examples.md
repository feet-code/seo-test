Examples make seasonal landscape service change tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small commercial landscaping and recurring property-maintenance companies can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: Weekly mowing shifts to leaf cleanup tasks

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer property and contract, Current service plan and version, Changed task frequency and season, then move it through open the seasonal change from contract review and define old and new service rules. If a contract reaches a seasonal transition, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Irrigation checks pause after winterization

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Current service plan and version, Changed task frequency and season, Effective and end dates, then move it through open the seasonal change from contract review and define old and new service rules. If the customer requests a temporary frequency change, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A retail property increases service before holiday traffic

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Changed task frequency and season, Effective and end dates, Route labor and material impact, then move it through open the seasonal change from contract review and define old and new service rules. If the first affected visit conflicts with the published plan, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open contract service change needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every seasonal contract change has an effective date, customer approval, route impact, crew acknowledgment, and verified first execution?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the landscape crm, contract, estimate, route, crew, job-cost, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
