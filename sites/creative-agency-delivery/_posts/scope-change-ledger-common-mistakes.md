Extra requests are accepted in calls and chat without a clear record of their effect on deliverables, time, fees, or deadlines. The recurring failures are usually process-design problems rather than motivation problems. For small creative, design, and digital agencies, these are the mistakes worth finding before buying or building software.


### 1. Calling a request small before estimating it

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Original deliverable** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: No extra work starts without a named decision owner When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 2. Letting work begin while approval is ambiguous

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Requested change** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Tradeoffs are offered alongside fees when useful When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 3. Recording price impact but not schedule impact

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Source and date** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: The delivery plan and invoice evidence reflect the same decision When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 4. Hiding agency-added gold plating inside client scope

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Impact on hours or fee** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Change is allowed; invisible change is not When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

## Audit five recent records

Pick five completed or abandoned examples and ask:

- Can we reconstruct client and project without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct original deliverable without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct requested change without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct source and date without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct impact on hours or fee without asking the original owner?

If the answer is no, improve the capture point rather than adding a later reporting step. Reports cannot recover decisions that were never recorded.

## Use mistakes as software requirements

Turn every frequent failure into a testable requirement. “Better visibility” is vague; “show every record with no owner or next date” can be tested. “More automation” is vague; “stop reminders after the completion condition is recorded” can be tested.
