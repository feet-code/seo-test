Examples make funeral home personal effects chain of custody easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent funeral homes and small death-care service teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A ring is removed and secured before preparation

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Case and decedent reference, Item description count and condition, Intake time location and staff, then move it through inventory items at the approved intake point and secure and label effects against the case. If personal effects enter or leave controlled storage, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Clothing is transferred to a service location

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Item description count and condition, Intake time location and staff, Container seal or storage location, then move it through inventory items at the approved intake point and secure and label effects against the case. If inventory and instruction records disagree, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A family representative collects documents and a device

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Intake time location and staff, Container seal or storage location, Transfer from to purpose and time, then move it through inventory items at the approved intake point and secure and label effects against the case. If a person requests release collection or disposition, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open personal-effects transfer needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every personal effect is inventoried with appropriate privacy, transferred through named custody events, and released or disposed only with authorized acknowledgment?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the funeral-home case, authorization, arrangement, scheduling, custody, and accounting platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
