A checklist for funeral home personal effects chain of custody should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent funeral homes and small death-care service teams and centers on one result: **every personal effect is inventoried with appropriate privacy, transferred through named custody events, and released or disposed only with authorized acknowledgment**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Case and decedent reference
- Confirm Item description count and condition
- Confirm Intake time location and staff
- Confirm Container seal or storage location

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Inventory items at the approved intake point
- Update Secure and label effects against the case
- Update Record each internal or external transfer
- Update Resolve instruction or identity discrepancies
- Update Release to an authorized recipient and preserve acknowledgment

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Transfer from to purpose and time
- Verify Family instruction and authorized decision
- Verify Exception investigation and reviewer
- Verify Recipient release acknowledgment and close time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where personal effects enter or leave controlled storage
- [ ] Review records where inventory and instruction records disagree
- [ ] Review records where a person requests release collection or disposition

- [ ] Check for using a general case note instead of item records
- [ ] Check for photographing sensitive effects more broadly than policy allows
- [ ] Check for moving an item with no receiving acknowledgment
- [ ] Check for releasing to a family member whose authority is not recorded

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Custody-record completeness, Exception resolution time, Release acknowledgment rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
