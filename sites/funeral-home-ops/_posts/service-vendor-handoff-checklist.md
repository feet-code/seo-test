A checklist for funeral service vendor and facility handoff tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent funeral homes and small death-care service teams and centers on one result: **every external service commitment has current instructions, accountable contacts, accepted timing, change history, and verified completion**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Case service and vendor
- Confirm Commitment scope date and location
- Confirm Primary and backup contacts
- Confirm Instructions privacy boundary and attachments

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Translate the authorized arrangement into vendor commitments
- Update Send only necessary instructions and deadlines
- Update Collect explicit acceptance or clarification
- Update Publish approved changes to affected parties
- Update Verify completion and reconcile invoices or exceptions

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Sent accepted and changed timestamps
- Verify Price deposit and invoice terms
- Verify Internal owner and escalation
- Verify Completion evidence exception and reconciliation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an authorized arrangement creates a vendor commitment
- [ ] Review records where time location or scope changes
- [ ] Review records where a vendor does not accept by the cutoff or reports a problem

- [ ] Check for sending more family data than the vendor needs
- [ ] Check for assuming an email attachment was accepted
- [ ] Check for changing timing with one vendor but not dependent parties
- [ ] Check for closing the commitment when the service begins

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Acceptance lead time, Late-change propagation, Vendor exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
