Examples make funeral service vendor and facility handoff tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent funeral homes and small death-care service teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A cemetery changes its arrival window

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Case service and vendor, Commitment scope date and location, Primary and backup contacts, then move it through translate the authorized arrangement into vendor commitments and send only necessary instructions and deadlines. If an authorized arrangement creates a vendor commitment, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A musician needs the final program order

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Commitment scope date and location, Primary and backup contacts, Instructions privacy boundary and attachments, then move it through translate the authorized arrangement into vendor commitments and send only necessary instructions and deadlines. If time location or scope changes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A florist delivery reaches the wrong entrance

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Primary and backup contacts, Instructions privacy boundary and attachments, Sent accepted and changed timestamps, then move it through translate the authorized arrangement into vendor commitments and send only necessary instructions and deadlines. If a vendor does not accept by the cutoff or reports a problem, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open service vendor commitment needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every external service commitment has current instructions, accountable contacts, accepted timing, change history, and verified completion?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the funeral-home case, authorization, arrangement, scheduling, custody, and accounting platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
