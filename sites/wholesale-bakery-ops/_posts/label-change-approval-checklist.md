A checklist for wholesale bakery allergen and label change approval should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small wholesale and direct-store-delivery bakeries and centers on one result: **every label change is reviewed by the responsible people, tied to effective product and lot boundaries, and verified at first production use**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Product SKU and customer variant
- Confirm Change source reason and requested date
- Confirm Old and new ingredient or recipe version
- Confirm Allergen nutrition claim and net-content impact

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open change from recipe supplier or requirement
- Update Assess ingredient allergen claim and package impact
- Update Review artwork data and customer variants
- Update Approve effective date lot and old-stock disposition
- Update Verify the first printed and applied production run

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Artwork file revision and printer
- Verify Reviewer roles and approvals
- Verify Effective lot date and obsolete-stock plan
- Verify First-run line check and evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an ingredient supplier recipe or claim changes
- [ ] Review records where a customer requests a private-label revision
- [ ] Review records where the first production check differs from approved artwork

- [ ] Check for changing artwork without linking the recipe version
- [ ] Check for assuming supplier substitution has no label effect
- [ ] Check for leaving old rolls accessible after effective lot
- [ ] Check for approving a pdf but skipping the applied-package check

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Change lead time, First-run accuracy, Obsolete-label variance. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
