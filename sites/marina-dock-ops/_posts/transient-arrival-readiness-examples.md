Examples make marina transient arrival readiness easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent marinas, yacht clubs, and small dock operations can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A sailboat draft conflicts with the assigned slip

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Marina, reservation, and boater, Vessel length, beam, draft, and power, Arrival and departure window, then move it through create readiness from the confirmed reservation and validate vessel, dates, services, and contact details. If a transient reservation is confirmed, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A late arrival needs after-hours gate instructions

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Vessel length, beam, draft, and power, Arrival and departure window, Assigned slip and compatibility checks, then move it through create readiness from the confirmed reservation and validate vessel, dates, services, and contact details. If vessel, timing, service, or slip availability changes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: Shore-power needs change after the dock team receives the plan

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Arrival and departure window, Assigned slip and compatibility checks, Utility and service requests, then move it through create readiness from the confirmed reservation and validate vessel, dates, services, and contact details. If a readiness field remains open near the arrival window, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open transient slip arrival needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every transient arrival has a compatible assigned slip, current instructions, payment plan, and acknowledged dock handoff?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the slip, reservation, boater, billing, utility, and maintenance platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
