A checklist for marina dock maintenance handoff should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent marinas, yacht clubs, and small dock operations and centers on one result: **every marina maintenance issue has contained impact, assigned repair, affected-slip communication, and verified return to service**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Marina, dock, slip, and asset
- Confirm Reported time and source
- Confirm Issue and impact
- Confirm Containment and affected slips

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the issue and exact dock location
- Update Assess impact and contain affected access or service
- Update Assign staff or contractor repair
- Update Communicate with affected boaters and operations
- Update Inspect completed work and restore availability

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Owner, contractor, and access plan
- Verify Parts, work, and ETA
- Verify Boater notice and temporary arrangement
- Verify Inspection evidence and restored time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a dock or boater reports a facility issue
- [ ] Review records where repair timing or impact changes affected slip availability
- [ ] Review records where contractor completion fails marina inspection

- [ ] Check for writing the issue against a dock with no exact asset
- [ ] Check for letting a contractor close work without marina inspection
- [ ] Check for moving a boat without updating slip and billing records
- [ ] Check for restoring availability before the containment is removed

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Containment time, Verified repair time, Reopen rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
