A checklist for marina transient arrival readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent marinas, yacht clubs, and small dock operations and centers on one result: **every transient arrival has a compatible assigned slip, current instructions, payment plan, and acknowledged dock handoff**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Marina, reservation, and boater
- Confirm Vessel length, beam, draft, and power
- Confirm Arrival and departure window
- Confirm Assigned slip and compatibility checks

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create readiness from the confirmed reservation
- Update Validate vessel, dates, services, and contact details
- Update Assign a compatible available slip
- Update Confirm access, utilities, arrival, and payment instructions
- Update Release the arrival plan to boater and dock team

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Utility and service requests
- Verify Balance and payment plan
- Verify Access and contact instructions
- Verify Dockhand owner and acknowledgment

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a transient reservation is confirmed
- [ ] Review records where vessel, timing, service, or slip availability changes
- [ ] Review records where a readiness field remains open near the arrival window

- [ ] Check for assigning by length without beam or utility fit
- [ ] Check for sending gate instructions before slip confirmation
- [ ] Check for changing the slip without updating the dock team
- [ ] Check for marking ready while arrival time and contact remain unknown

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-before-arrival rate, Slip reassignment rate, Arrival wait time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
