The most useful fitness studio trial follow-up template is a small operating record. It should answer what is happening, who owns it, what evidence exists, and when the next decision occurs. This structure works in a spreadsheet, database, or focused application.

## Recommended record fields

| Field | Why it exists | Update point |

## Suggested statuses

Use workflow statuses that describe reality: **Capture The Trial Source And Goal → Confirm Attendance And Class Experience → Assign The Post Visit Follow Up → Handle Schedule Fit And Pricing Questions → Convert Nurture With Consent Or Close**. Add **Waiting** only when you also capture a waiting reason and review date. Add **Closed—Not Completed** when an item legitimately ends without the desired outcome.

## Follow-up rules

- When an attended trial has no follow-up by the target time, assign a next action and review date.
- When a prospect asks about a class, injury accommodation, or price, assign a next action and review date.
- When a membership purchase or opt-out arrives through another channel, assign a next action and review date.

Avoid reminders with no stop condition. A rule should say when it starts, who receives it, what counts as a response, and when a person should take over.

## Example records

- A first-time Pilates visitor asks about beginner times
- A guest pass attends but the booking profile has no phone consent
- A prospect buys online before the front desk sends the planned follow-up

For each example, write the current status, next action, owner, and supporting evidence. This makes the template testable with real work rather than idealized sample data.

## Quality-control rules

- Every open trial-member opportunity needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every attended trial receives one relevant membership next step or a documented no-contact outcome
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep studio booking and membership platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

Before adding automation, run the template manually for a week. Remove ambiguous fields and confirm that two different users classify the same situation the same way. Consistency matters more than having a long form.
