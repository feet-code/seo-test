Trial visitors enter through forms, class bookings, guest passes, and walk-ins, but staff cannot see who attended, what they wanted, or whether someone followed up. The recurring failures are usually process-design problems rather than motivation problems. For boutique fitness studios and group-class operators, these are the mistakes worth finding before buying or building software.


### 1. Following up before confirming whether the person attended

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Lead source** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Completion requires recorded evidence that every attended trial receives one relevant membership next step or a documented no-contact outcome When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 2. Sending a generic discount that ignores the stated goal

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Goal and class interest** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 3. Counting a second free class as a paid conversion

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Trial booking and attendance** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Keep studio booking and membership platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 4. Continuing marketing after consent is withdrawn

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Instructor observation** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Every open trial-member opportunity needs one owner and a next review time When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

## Audit five recent records

Pick five completed or abandoned examples and ask:

- Can we reconstruct prospect and preferred channel without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct lead source without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct goal and class interest without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct trial booking and attendance without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct instructor observation without asking the original owner?

If the answer is no, improve the capture point rather than adding a later reporting step. Reports cannot recover decisions that were never recorded.

## Use mistakes as software requirements

Turn every frequent failure into a testable requirement. “Better visibility” is vague; “show every record with no owner or next date” can be tested. “More automation” is vague; “stop reminders after the completion condition is recorded” can be tested.
