A checklist for fitness studio trial follow-up should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for boutique fitness studios and group-class operators and centers on one result: **every attended trial receives one relevant membership next step or a documented no-contact outcome**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Prospect and preferred channel
- Confirm Lead source
- Confirm Goal and class interest
- Confirm Trial booking and attendance

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the trial source and goal
- Update Confirm attendance and class experience
- Update Assign the post-visit follow-up
- Update Handle schedule, fit, and pricing questions
- Update Convert, nurture with consent, or close

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Instructor observation
- Verify Follow-up owner and due time
- Verify Question or objection
- Verify Membership outcome or closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an attended trial has no follow-up by the target time
- [ ] Review records where a prospect asks about a class, injury accommodation, or price
- [ ] Review records where a membership purchase or opt-out arrives through another channel

- [ ] Check for following up before confirming whether the person attended
- [ ] Check for sending a generic discount that ignores the stated goal
- [ ] Check for counting a second free class as a paid conversion
- [ ] Check for continuing marketing after consent is withdrawn

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Attended-trial conversion, First follow-up time, Unresolved trial age. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
