Trial visitors enter through forms, class bookings, guest passes, and walk-ins, but staff cannot see who attended, what they wanted, or whether someone followed up. For boutique fitness studios and group-class operators, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every attended trial receives one relevant membership next step or a documented no-contact outcome**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open trial-member opportunity needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every attended trial receives one relevant membership next step or a documented no-contact outcome
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep studio booking and membership platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Capture the trial source and goal

Record **Prospect and preferred channel** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can confirm attendance and class experience, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Confirm attendance and class experience

Record **Lead source** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can assign the post-visit follow-up, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Assign the post-visit follow-up

Record **Goal and class interest** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can handle schedule, fit, and pricing questions, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Handle schedule, fit, and pricing questions

Record **Trial booking and attendance** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can convert, nurture with consent, or close, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Convert, nurture with consent, or close

Record **Instructor observation** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- an attended trial has no follow-up by the target time
- a prospect asks about a class, injury accommodation, or price
- a membership purchase or opt-out arrives through another channel

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
