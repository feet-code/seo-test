Examples make restaurant prep shortage tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent restaurants and small multi-location restaurant groups can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: Sauce yield is half the dinner par

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Location, shift, and station, Prep item and unit, Par, on-hand, and expected demand, then move it through identify the shortage against the service plan and quantify available and required amount. If verified quantity falls below service demand, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A delivery shortage forces an approved garnish substitute

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Prep item and unit, Par, on-hand, and expected demand, Affected menu items, then move it through identify the shortage against the service plan and quantify available and required amount. If the recovery action misses its ready-by time, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: Additional prep will finish after the first reservation wave

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Par, on-hand, and expected demand, Affected menu items, Shortage cause, then move it through identify the shortage against the service plan and quantify available and required amount. If a substitution or outage changes guest-facing availability, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open prep shortage needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every service-impacting prep shortage has a quantified gap, approved response, owner, and communicated menu consequence?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the pos, inventory, recipe, scheduling, and maintenance systems as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
