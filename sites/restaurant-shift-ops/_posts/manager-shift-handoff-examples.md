Examples make restaurant manager shift handoff tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent restaurants and small multi-location restaurant groups can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A guest recovery call is due tomorrow morning

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Location, date, and shift, Issue category and description, Guest, order, equipment, or vendor reference, then move it through capture the unresolved issue during the shift and classify impact and immediate containment. If a shift ends with unresolved work, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: The ice machine repair has a vendor ETA but no verification

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Issue category and description, Guest, order, equipment, or vendor reference, Impact and containment, then move it through capture the unresolved issue during the shift and classify impact and immediate containment. If an issue affects the next shift's service or staffing, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A staffing callout remains uncovered for brunch

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Guest, order, equipment, or vendor reference, Impact and containment, Current owner, then move it through capture the unresolved issue during the shift and classify impact and immediate containment. If a promised update or vendor response becomes overdue, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open shift issue needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every unresolved shift issue transfers with impact, owner, next action, due time, and explicit acceptance by the next manager?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the pos, inventory, recipe, scheduling, and maintenance systems as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
