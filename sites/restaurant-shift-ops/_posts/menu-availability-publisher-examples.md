Examples make restaurant 86 list and menu availability tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent restaurants and small multi-location restaurant groups can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: Salmon sells out but remains on a delivery marketplace

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Location and shift, Menu item or modifier, Reason and remaining quantity, then move it through open the item availability change and confirm item, modifier, location, and expected duration. If an item cannot support expected demand, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: One sauce modifier makes two dishes unavailable

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Menu item or modifier, Reason and remaining quantity, Unavailable-from and expected return, then move it through open the item availability change and confirm item, modifier, location, and expected duration. If one channel differs from the approved availability state, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A produce delivery arrives and the chef verifies the item can return

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Reason and remaining quantity, Unavailable-from and expected return, Affected channels, then move it through open the item availability change and confirm item, modifier, location, and expected duration. If verified supply returns or the expected return time passes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open menu availability change needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every availability change is approved, published to all intended channels, acknowledged by service staff, and reversed only after supply is verified?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the pos, inventory, recipe, scheduling, and maintenance systems as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
