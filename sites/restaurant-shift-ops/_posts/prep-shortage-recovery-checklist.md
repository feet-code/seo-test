A checklist for restaurant prep shortage tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent restaurants and small multi-location restaurant groups and centers on one result: **every service-impacting prep shortage has a quantified gap, approved response, owner, and communicated menu consequence**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Location, shift, and station
- Confirm Prep item and unit
- Confirm Par, on-hand, and expected demand
- Confirm Affected menu items

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Identify the shortage against the service plan
- Update Quantify available and required amount
- Update Choose additional prep, substitution, purchase, or menu action
- Update Assign and execute the recovery
- Update Verify supply and communicate the final status

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Shortage cause
- Verify Approved recovery action
- Verify Owner and ready-by time
- Verify Verified quantity and communication

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where verified quantity falls below service demand
- [ ] Review records where the recovery action misses its ready-by time
- [ ] Review records where a substitution or outage changes guest-facing availability

- [ ] Check for calling low without a quantity
- [ ] Check for substituting an ingredient without authorized recipe review
- [ ] Check for sending staff to purchase before comparing demand
- [ ] Check for closing when work starts rather than when supply is verified

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Recovery cycle time, Shortage frequency, Recovered-before-impact rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
