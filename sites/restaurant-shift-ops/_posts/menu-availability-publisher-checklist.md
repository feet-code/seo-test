A checklist for restaurant 86 list and menu availability tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent restaurants and small multi-location restaurant groups and centers on one result: **every availability change is approved, published to all intended channels, acknowledged by service staff, and reversed only after supply is verified**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Location and shift
- Confirm Menu item or modifier
- Confirm Reason and remaining quantity
- Confirm Unavailable-from and expected return

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the item availability change
- Update Confirm item, modifier, location, and expected duration
- Update Approve guest-facing wording and alternatives
- Update Publish across POS, online, and team channels
- Update Verify live state and schedule reactivation review

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Affected channels
- Verify Approved alternative or message
- Verify Publisher and verification evidence
- Verify Reactivation owner and time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an item cannot support expected demand
- [ ] Review records where one channel differs from the approved availability state
- [ ] Review records where verified supply returns or the expected return time passes

- [ ] Check for 86ing the parent item but not affected modifiers
- [ ] Check for updating the pos but not online channels
- [ ] Check for un-86ing from an expected delivery rather than verified stock
- [ ] Check for leaving servers to discover the change from failed orders

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Channel consistency time, Availability correction rate, Guest-impact orders. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
