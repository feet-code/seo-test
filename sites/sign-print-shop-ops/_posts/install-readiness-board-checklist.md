A checklist for sign installation readiness tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent sign shops, commercial printers, and display fabricators and centers on one result: **every installation dispatch has verified product, site, permission, equipment, crew, and customer readiness**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer, job, and site
- Confirm Approved proof and fabricated items
- Confirm Survey dimensions and mounting condition
- Confirm Permit or landlord approval

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open install readiness from the approved job
- Update Verify fabrication, packaging, and hardware
- Update Confirm site survey, permit, access, and customer window
- Update Match qualified crew and equipment
- Update Run final dispatch review and release the install packet

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Access contact and install window
- Verify Crew skills and assignments
- Verify Vehicle, lift, tools, and hardware
- Verify Weather decision and dispatch release

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an installation approaches dispatch cutoff
- [ ] Review records where fabrication, permit, site, crew, equipment, or weather changes
- [ ] Review records where final review finds a mismatch with the approved job

- [ ] Check for scheduling from an expected fabrication date
- [ ] Check for sending crew without the approved site-contact window
- [ ] Check for loading the sign but not mounting hardware
- [ ] Check for assuming a prior survey still reflects site conditions

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-by-dispatch rate, Abort or return-trip rate, Site-wait time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
