A checklist for print and sign proof approval tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent sign shops, commercial printers, and display fabricators and centers on one result: **every job enters production only from an exact proof version approved by the authorized customer contact**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer, job, and line item
- Confirm Artwork and proof version
- Confirm Dimensions, substrate, color, and finish
- Confirm Approver and deadline

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Generate the proof from the current job specification
- Update Send it to the named approver with deadline
- Update Capture image-specific or page-specific corrections
- Update Issue a new controlled proof version
- Update Record final approval and release that version to production

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Corrections and annotation
- Verify Revision owner
- Verify Approval evidence and time
- Verify Production-release version

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a proof reaches its response deadline
- [ ] Review records where customer corrections create a new version
- [ ] Review records where production receives artwork different from the approved proof

- [ ] Check for accepting looks good without identifying the proof
- [ ] Check for overwriting artwork after approval
- [ ] Check for letting sales release a job from an email attachment
- [ ] Check for starting one line item because another item in the job was approved

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Proof approval cycle, Revision count, Post-approval correction rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
