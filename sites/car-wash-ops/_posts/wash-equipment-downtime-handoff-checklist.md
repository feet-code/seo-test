A checklist for car wash equipment downtime tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent express, tunnel, and multi-bay car wash operators and centers on one result: **every equipment incident has contained customer impact, named repair ownership, shift-to-shift status, and verified return to service**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Location asset and component
- Confirm Reported time source and symptoms
- Confirm Customer and operating impact
- Confirm Containment and signage

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the asset fault and operating impact
- Update Contain the affected lane bay or feature
- Update Diagnose and assign internal or vendor action
- Update Transfer status at each shift handoff
- Update Test repair and restore the exact capability

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Diagnostics error codes and photos
- Verify Owner vendor part and ETA
- Verify Shift handoff next action and review time
- Verify Test evidence restored capability and time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where equipment or staff reports a wash-impacting fault
- [ ] Review records where repair eta or capability changes the customer plan
- [ ] Review records where completed work fails site testing

- [ ] Check for writing down only machine down
- [ ] Check for keeping a lane open with an undocumented degraded feature
- [ ] Check for letting a vendor close work without wash-site testing
- [ ] Check for removing signage before the containment is cleared

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Containment time, Verified downtime, Repeat-fault rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
