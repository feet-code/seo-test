A checklist for hotel guest maintenance handoff should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent boutique hotels and small hospitality teams and centers on one result: **every guest-impacting maintenance issue has a coordinated access plan, verified repair, and completed guest follow-up**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Guest, stay, and room
- Confirm Issue and reported time
- Confirm Impact and urgency
- Confirm Permission and access window

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the issue and guest impact
- Update Triage urgency, room status, and access
- Update Assign repair and communicate the next update
- Update Verify the fix in the room
- Update Follow up with the guest and reconcile room status

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Owner, vendor, and next update
- Verify Work performed and parts
- Verify Verification evidence
- Verify Guest response, recovery, and room status

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an in-house guest reports a room defect
- [ ] Review records where repair cannot meet the communicated update or requires a room move
- [ ] Review records where the technician closes work but room verification fails

- [ ] Check for sending engineering without confirming room access
- [ ] Check for marking fixed when a technician leaves
- [ ] Check for moving the guest without updating room and maintenance status
- [ ] Check for promising an exact repair time before parts are confirmed

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are First-owner time, Verified resolution time, Guest follow-up completion. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
