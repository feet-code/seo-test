Examples make hotel lost and found claim tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent boutique hotels and small hospitality teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A guest describes an engraved ring without seeing the inventory record

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Hotel, room area, and found time, Item category and nonpublic identifiers, Finder and custody events, then move it through register the found item without exposing identifying detail and record the guest claim and verification answers. If a new claim may match an existing found item, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A charger moves from housekeeping to secured storage

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Item category and nonpublic identifiers, Finder and custody events, Storage location, then move it through register the found item without exposing identifying detail and record the guest claim and verification answers. If an item changes storage location or custodian, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A shipped passport cannot use the hotel's normal courier process

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Finder and custody events, Storage location, Claimant and stay reference, then move it through register the found item without exposing identifying detail and record the guest claim and verification answers. If retention expires or pickup and shipping arrangements fail, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open lost-property claim needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every found item and guest claim is matched, released, retained, or disposed under policy with a complete custody trail?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep pms, room-status, maintenance, and guest-service systems as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
