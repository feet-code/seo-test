A checklist for hotel lost and found claim tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent boutique hotels and small hospitality teams and centers on one result: **every found item and guest claim is matched, released, retained, or disposed under policy with a complete custody trail**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Hotel, room area, and found time
- Confirm Item category and nonpublic identifiers
- Confirm Finder and custody events
- Confirm Storage location

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the found item without exposing identifying detail
- Update Record the guest claim and verification answers
- Update Match claims to inventory under controlled review
- Update Arrange pickup or approved shipping
- Update Record release, retention, or disposal

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Claimant and stay reference
- Verify Verification questions and match decision
- Verify Pickup or shipping authorization
- Verify Release recipient or policy disposition

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a new claim may match an existing found item
- [ ] Review records where an item changes storage location or custodian
- [ ] Review records where retention expires or pickup and shipping arrangements fail

- [ ] Check for publishing distinctive item details before verifying the claimant
- [ ] Check for moving an item without a custody event
- [ ] Check for shipping before payment and address authorization are clear
- [ ] Check for deleting unmatched records before the retention period ends

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Claim resolution time, Custody completeness, Verified return rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
