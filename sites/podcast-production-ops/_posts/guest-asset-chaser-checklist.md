A checklist for podcast guest asset and release tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent podcast producers and small branded-podcast teams and centers on one result: **every scheduled guest has the minimum approved assets and permissions needed for recording, publishing, and promotion**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Show, episode, and guest
- Confirm Recording and publish dates
- Confirm Biography and pronunciation
- Confirm Headshot and usage permission

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create the guest asset packet from the episode plan
- Update Request only the required files and permissions
- Update Validate submissions and resolve gaps
- Update Freeze the production-ready asset set
- Update Publish, share, and archive final evidence

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Release or consent status
- Verify Topics and links
- Verify Promotion restrictions and handles
- Verify Asset owner, status, and approved version

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a scheduled guest is missing a blocking item near the cutoff
- [ ] Review records where a submitted asset fails format, permission, or content checks
- [ ] Review records where the episode scope or publication date changes required assets

- [ ] Check for requesting every possible asset before the guest confirms
- [ ] Check for using an image without explicit source or permission
- [ ] Check for changing the bio during editing without guest approval
- [ ] Check for chasing promotion after the guest declined that expectation

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-before-record rate, Asset revision count, Publication hold time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
