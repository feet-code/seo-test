A recording can be booked while biography, headshot, pronunciation, release, talking points, links, and promotion approvals remain scattered across guest emails. For independent podcast producers and small branded-podcast teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every scheduled guest has the minimum approved assets and permissions needed for recording, publishing, and promotion**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open guest asset requirement needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every scheduled guest has the minimum approved assets and permissions needed for recording, publishing, and promotion
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep episode plan, release archive, and production workspace as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Create the guest asset packet from the episode plan

Record **Show, episode, and guest** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can request only the required files and permissions, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Request only the required files and permissions

Record **Recording and publish dates** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can validate submissions and resolve gaps, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Validate submissions and resolve gaps

Record **Biography and pronunciation** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can freeze the production-ready asset set, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Freeze the production-ready asset set

Record **Headshot and usage permission** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can publish, share, and archive final evidence, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Publish, share, and archive final evidence

Record **Release or consent status** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a scheduled guest is missing a blocking item near the cutoff
- a submitted asset fails format, permission, or content checks
- the episode scope or publication date changes required assets

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
