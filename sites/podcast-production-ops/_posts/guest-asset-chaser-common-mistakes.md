A recording can be booked while biography, headshot, pronunciation, release, talking points, links, and promotion approvals remain scattered across guest emails. The recurring failures are usually process-design problems rather than motivation problems. For independent podcast producers and small branded-podcast teams, these are the mistakes worth finding before buying or building software.


### 1. Requesting every possible asset before the guest confirms

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Recording and publish dates** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Completion requires recorded evidence that every scheduled guest has the minimum approved assets and permissions needed for recording, publishing, and promotion When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 2. Using an image without explicit source or permission

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Biography and pronunciation** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 3. Changing the bio during editing without guest approval

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Headshot and usage permission** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Keep episode plan, release archive, and production workspace as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 4. Chasing promotion after the guest declined that expectation

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Release or consent status** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Every open guest asset requirement needs one owner and a next review time When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

## Audit five recent records

Pick five completed or abandoned examples and ask:

- Can we reconstruct show, episode, and guest without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct recording and publish dates without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct biography and pronunciation without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct headshot and usage permission without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct release or consent status without asking the original owner?

If the answer is no, improve the capture point rather than adding a later reporting step. Reports cannot recover decisions that were never recorded.

## Use mistakes as software requirements

Turn every frequent failure into a testable requirement. “Better visibility” is vague; “show every record with no owner or next date” can be tested. “More automation” is vague; “stop reminders after the completion condition is recorded” can be tested.
