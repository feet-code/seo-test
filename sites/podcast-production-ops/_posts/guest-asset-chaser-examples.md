Examples make podcast guest asset and release tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent podcast producers and small branded-podcast teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A guest submits a headshot with no known usage rights

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Show, episode, and guest, Recording and publish dates, Biography and pronunciation, then move it through create the guest asset packet from the episode plan and request only the required files and permissions. If a scheduled guest is missing a blocking item near the cutoff, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. If a submitted asset fails format, permission, or content checks, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A title change requires the guest bio and promotional copy to be reapproved

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Biography and pronunciation, Headshot and usage permission, Release or consent status, then move it through create the guest asset packet from the episode plan and request only the required files and permissions. If the episode scope or publication date changes required assets, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. ## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open guest asset requirement needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every scheduled guest has the minimum approved assets and permissions needed for recording, publishing, and promotion?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep episode plan, release archive, and production workspace as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
