A checklist for music school makeup lesson credit tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent music schools and multi-teacher lesson studios and centers on one result: **every eligible missed lesson becomes one scheduled makeup, valid credit, policy closure, or billing adjustment with a clear expiration**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Student, family, and instrument
- Confirm Original lesson and teacher
- Confirm Cancellation party and notice time
- Confirm Policy version and eligibility

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Record the missed lesson and cancellation source
- Update Apply the current studio policy
- Update Create the makeup option or credit
- Update Confirm attendance or alternate resolution
- Update Reconcile schedule, credit, teacher pay, and billing

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Credit type, value, and expiry
- Verify Offered makeup options
- Verify Confirmed session and attendance
- Verify Teacher pay and billing reconciliation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an eligible missed lesson has no resolution
- [ ] Review records where a credit approaches expiry
- [ ] Review records where a scheduled makeup is canceled or conflicts with teacher eligibility

- [ ] Check for giving a credit without linking the original lesson
- [ ] Check for applying different policy based on who answers the message
- [ ] Check for using the same credit for two rescheduled lessons
- [ ] Check for leaving teacher compensation unresolved after a studio cancellation

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Credit resolution time, Expiring-credit backlog, Reconciliation correction rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
