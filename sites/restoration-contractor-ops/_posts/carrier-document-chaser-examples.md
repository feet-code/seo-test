Examples make restoration insurance document tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small water, fire, and property-restoration contractors can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: An adjuster requests daily drying logs and equipment photos

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Job, claim, and carrier, Adjuster and communication channel, Requested artifact and scope, then move it through register the carrier request and claim reference and identify the source artifact and owner. If a carrier request approaches its due date, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A supplement is returned because the estimate and invoice versions differ

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Adjuster and communication channel, Requested artifact and scope, Due date and dependency, then move it through register the carrier request and claim reference and identify the source artifact and owner. If a submission lacks acknowledgment by the review threshold, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A portal upload succeeds but no receipt appears in the claim

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Requested artifact and scope, Due date and dependency, Document owner and reviewer, then move it through register the carrier request and claim reference and identify the source artifact and owner. If the adjuster rejects, questions, or changes the required scope, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open carrier document request needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every carrier document request has a defined artifact, owner, submitted version, acknowledgment, and resolved response?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep job file, field-documentation, estimating, and carrier systems as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
