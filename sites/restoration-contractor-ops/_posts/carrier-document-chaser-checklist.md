A checklist for restoration insurance document tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small water, fire, and property-restoration contractors and centers on one result: **every carrier document request has a defined artifact, owner, submitted version, acknowledgment, and resolved response**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Job, claim, and carrier
- Confirm Adjuster and communication channel
- Confirm Requested artifact and scope
- Confirm Due date and dependency

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the carrier request and claim reference
- Update Identify the source artifact and owner
- Update Prepare and quality-check the package
- Update Submit through the required channel
- Update Track acknowledgment, questions, and acceptance

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Document owner and reviewer
- Verify Submitted version and time
- Verify Carrier acknowledgment and question
- Verify Accepted outcome or resubmission

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a carrier request approaches its due date
- [ ] Review records where a submission lacks acknowledgment by the review threshold
- [ ] Review records where the adjuster rejects, questions, or changes the required scope

- [ ] Check for sending a folder link without identifying the requested artifact
- [ ] Check for resubmitting a corrected estimate under the same version name
- [ ] Check for counting sent email as carrier acceptance
- [ ] Check for letting sensitive documents remain accessible after the exchange

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Request turnaround, First-submission acceptance, Unacknowledged submission age. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
