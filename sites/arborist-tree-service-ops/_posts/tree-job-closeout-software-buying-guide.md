Software for tree service cleanup and stump closeout tracking should be evaluated against the operating problem, not a generic feature checklist. For independent arborist, pruning, removal, and plant-health-care companies, a useful trial must demonstrate this outcome: **every tree job closes with contracted scope, site cleanup, retained materials, follow-on work, and customer-facing evidence reconciled**.

## Write requirements from the workflow

The tool must support these steps without hidden spreadsheets: Compare completed work with current scope, Inspect cleanup property and retained materials, Record deferred stump or follow-on work, Complete customer and permit handoff, Release billing after closeout review. It must also make these fields easy to capture at the moment work happens: Customer property and job, Tree and completed scope, Before and after photos, Debris wood and chip disposition, Stump treatment and follow-on date, Property or access condition, Customer walkthrough and exception, Reviewer billing release and close time.

## Use a live demo script

Ask the vendor—or your internal prototype—to complete these tasks:

- Create and resolve this test case: Logs promised to the homeowner are hauled away
- Create and resolve this test case: Stump grinding is sold but scheduled separately
- Create and resolve this test case: A gate is damaged during debris removal

Then test one waiting case, one reassignment, one closed-without-completion case, and one export. Do not accept a slide deck in place of the workflow.

## Score the trial

| Metric | Simple calculation | Decision it supports |
|---|---|---|
| First-pass closeout rate | jobs passing review without correction / jobs reviewed | improve crew checklist |
| Deferred-work age | current date - deferred task created | manage stump backlog |
| Post-close complaint rate | jobs with cleanup or scope complaint / jobs closed | strengthen walkthrough |

Add setup time, recurring administration, export quality, permission clarity, and mobile usability where relevant. Weight the score by frequency: a daily two-minute annoyance matters more than a rare advanced feature.

## Red flags

- Treating crew departure as job completion
- Forgetting customer-requested wood or chips
- Closing removal while stump work has no owner
- Sending the final invoice before damage or permit conditions are reviewed

Also be cautious when the product requires broad process migration before it can solve the narrow problem, or when basic history/export controls are unavailable.

## Make the decision with real records

Run a small trial using current work, not sanitized sample data. Compare the realistic alternatives below and record why the winning approach fits now:

| Approach | Best when | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Estimate notes, site photos, permit emails, crew texts, and paper job packets | One owner handles low volume and can see every open item | Status and follow-up history depend on memory and inbox searches |
| Tree-service software or a shared field-readiness board | The team already maintains it and exceptions are simple | Purpose-built reminders, evidence, and stop conditions require manual setup |
| A focused workflow tool | The same coordination failure repeats across many live records | It must integrate with the system of record and justify another workflow |
