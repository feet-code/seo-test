A checklist for tree service cleanup and stump closeout tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent arborist, pruning, removal, and plant-health-care companies and centers on one result: **every tree job closes with contracted scope, site cleanup, retained materials, follow-on work, and customer-facing evidence reconciled**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and job
- Confirm Tree and completed scope
- Confirm Before and after photos
- Confirm Debris wood and chip disposition

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Compare completed work with current scope
- Update Inspect cleanup property and retained materials
- Update Record deferred stump or follow-on work
- Update Complete customer and permit handoff
- Update Release billing after closeout review

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Stump treatment and follow-on date
- Verify Property or access condition
- Verify Customer walkthrough and exception
- Verify Reviewer billing release and close time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where the field crew marks primary work complete
- [ ] Review records where closeout finds deferred scope damage or retained material
- [ ] Review records where a follow-on task misses its promised date

- [ ] Check for treating crew departure as job completion
- [ ] Check for forgetting customer-requested wood or chips
- [ ] Check for closing removal while stump work has no owner
- [ ] Check for sending the final invoice before damage or permit conditions are reviewed

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are First-pass closeout rate, Deferred-work age, Post-close complaint rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
