Examples make tree service permit utility and site readiness easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent arborist, pruning, removal, and plant-health-care companies can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A street tree needs a city permit

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer property and job, Tree location species and scope, Permit status and conditions, then move it through review accepted work and tree inventory and confirm permit utility and property permissions. If an accepted job enters the schedule, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Overhead lines require documented coordination

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Tree location species and scope, Permit status and conditions, Utility proximity and coordination, then move it through review accepted work and tree inventory and confirm permit utility and property permissions. If permit utility access or scope status changes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A crane setup area is no longer accessible

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Permit status and conditions, Utility proximity and coordination, Site access staging and traffic control, then move it through review accepted work and tree inventory and confirm permit utility and property permissions. If crew review finds a missing readiness condition, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open tree job readiness record needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every tree job is released only with site-specific scope, permissions, hazards, equipment, and access verified for the assigned crew?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the tree-service crm, estimate, tree inventory, schedule, permit, and job platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
