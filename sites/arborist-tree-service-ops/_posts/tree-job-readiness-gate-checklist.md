A checklist for tree service permit utility and site readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent arborist, pruning, removal, and plant-health-care companies and centers on one result: **every tree job is released only with site-specific scope, permissions, hazards, equipment, and access verified for the assigned crew**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and job
- Confirm Tree location species and scope
- Confirm Permit status and conditions
- Confirm Utility proximity and coordination

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Review accepted work and tree inventory
- Update Confirm permit utility and property permissions
- Update Validate hazards access and equipment plan
- Update Resolve readiness exceptions
- Update Release the current job packet to the crew

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Site access staging and traffic control
- Verify Crew qualification equipment and disposal plan
- Verify Customer neighbor and timing constraints
- Verify Reviewer release time and packet version

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an accepted job enters the schedule
- [ ] Review records where permit utility access or scope status changes
- [ ] Review records where crew review finds a missing readiness condition

- [ ] Check for assuming a customer approval satisfies a local permit
- [ ] Check for copying utility notes from another tree
- [ ] Check for scheduling crane or chipper access without site verification
- [ ] Check for letting the crew use an estimate superseded by a change

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-by-dispatch rate, Onsite readiness failure, Packet-version accuracy. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
