A checklist for environmental chain of custody exception tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small environmental consulting and field-sampling teams and centers on one result: **every custody discrepancy is contained, reviewed by qualified personnel, linked to affected samples, and resolved without rewriting original evidence**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Project event shipment and cooler
- Confirm Sample IDs containers and requested analyses
- Confirm Collector transfer receiver and timestamps
- Confirm Seal condition temperature and preservation

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the discrepancy at transfer or receipt
- Update Contain and identify affected samples
- Update Compare original field transfer and laboratory evidence
- Update Obtain qualified disposition or clarification
- Update Preserve correction linkage and final sample status

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Original custody form and label images
- Verify Discrepancy type affected samples and impact
- Verify Qualified reviewer disposition and rationale
- Verify Laboratory status correction link and notification

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where field or laboratory staff detects a custody mismatch
- [ ] Review records where hold time or sample condition makes review urgent
- [ ] Review records where clarification changes laboratory acceptance or reporting status

- [ ] Check for editing the original custody timestamp
- [ ] Check for guessing which sample a loose label belongs to
- [ ] Check for treating a clarification email as invisible metadata
- [ ] Check for allowing software to decide sample usability without qualified review

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Exception detection time, Qualified decision time, Repeat discrepancy rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
