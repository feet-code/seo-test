Examples make environmental chain of custody exception tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small environmental consulting and field-sampling teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A cooler arrives with one fewer container than the form

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Project event shipment and cooler, Sample IDs containers and requested analyses, Collector transfer receiver and timestamps, then move it through register the discrepancy at transfer or receipt and contain and identify affected samples. If field or laboratory staff detects a custody mismatch, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A transfer signature lacks a time

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Sample IDs containers and requested analyses, Collector transfer receiver and timestamps, Seal condition temperature and preservation, then move it through register the discrepancy at transfer or receipt and contain and identify affected samples. If hold time or sample condition makes review urgent, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A label ID differs by one character from the custody record

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Collector transfer receiver and timestamps, Seal condition temperature and preservation, Original custody form and label images, then move it through register the discrepancy at transfer or receipt and contain and identify affected samples. If clarification changes laboratory acceptance or reporting status, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open sample custody exception needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every custody discrepancy is contained, reviewed by qualified personnel, linked to affected samples, and resolved without rewriting original evidence?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the environmental project, sampling plan, field form, sample, laboratory, and reporting platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
