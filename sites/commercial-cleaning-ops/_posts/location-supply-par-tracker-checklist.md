A checklist for janitorial supply inventory and location replenishment tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for owner-operated commercial cleaning and janitorial companies and centers on one result: **each location has enough approved supplies for the next service window without uncontrolled overstock**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client location
- Confirm Storage area
- Confirm Item and unit
- Confirm Approved product
- Confirm Par level

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Define the item and par level
- Update Count usable stock
- Update Calculate the replenishment need
- Update Place and track the order
- Update Confirm location delivery

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Usable on hand
- Verify Count date
- Verify Reorder quantity
- Verify Order owner
- Verify Delivery evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where usable stock falls below the reorder point
- [ ] Review records where usage changes sharply from the prior count
- [ ] Review records where an approved item is unavailable or substituted

- [ ] Check for mixing cases, rolls, and individual units
- [ ] Check for counting damaged or inaccessible stock as usable
- [ ] Check for using one par level for locations with different service patterns
- [ ] Check for marking delivered when supplies reached the office rather than the site

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Stockout event count, Inventory days above par, Replenishment lead time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
