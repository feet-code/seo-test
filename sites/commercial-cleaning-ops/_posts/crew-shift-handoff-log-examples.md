Examples make commercial cleaning shift handoff and crew communication logs easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases owner-operated commercial cleaning and janitorial companies can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A locked office prevents one scheduled task and the day crew needs to retry

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Client location, Shift and timestamp, Area, then move it through capture the exception at the site and classify whether work is complete or blocked. If a task cannot be completed during the assigned shift, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A floor machine fails after part of the area is complete

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Shift and timestamp, Area, Exception type, then move it through capture the exception at the site and classify whether work is complete or blocked. If access, equipment, or supply conditions block work, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A spill is cleaned but damaged material requires client follow-up

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Area, Exception type, Description, then move it through capture the exception at the site and classify whether work is complete or blocked. If an issue could affect the client before the next routine review, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make a handoff describes unfinished reality, not blame?
- Did the record make every unresolved item has one next owner?
- Did the record make urgent client-impacting exceptions follow a separate escalation path?
- Did the record make resolution preserves what was done and verified?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
