A checklist for veterinary lab result callback tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent veterinary clinics and small client-service teams and centers on one result: **every expected result is reviewed by the assigned clinician and communicated to the client with a documented next step**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Patient and client
- Confirm Test and specimen date
- Confirm Expected result date
- Confirm Result received time

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the expected result and owner
- Update Confirm the result has arrived
- Update Queue clinician interpretation
- Update Communicate the approved summary to the client
- Update Record acknowledgment and next action

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Reviewing clinician
- Verify Review status and priority
- Verify Client contact evidence
- Verify Next action or closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a result arrives without clinician review in the target window
- [ ] Review records where the reviewing clinician requests an urgent client callback
- [ ] Review records where the ordering clinician is unavailable or the client cannot be reached

- [ ] Check for counting result receipt as client notification
- [ ] Check for letting administrative staff interpret an unreviewed result
- [ ] Check for sending repeated messages after a callback is acknowledged
- [ ] Check for losing responsibility when the ordering clinician is away

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Result-to-review time, Review-to-client time, Unresolved result age. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
