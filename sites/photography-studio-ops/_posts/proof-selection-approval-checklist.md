A checklist for photography client proof selection and approval should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent photography studios and small commercial photo teams and centers on one result: **every client selection and approval identifies the exact images, revision, intended output, approver, and final decision**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client, shoot, and gallery
- Confirm Proof-set version
- Confirm Image identifier
- Confirm Selection status and intended output

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Publish the controlled proof set
- Update Collect image-specific selections and comments
- Update Confirm product, usage, and retouching scope
- Update Return revisions for approval when required
- Update Freeze approved finals and release production

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Client comment
- Verify Retouching or crop request
- Verify Authorized approver and decision time
- Verify Final version and production release

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a proof deadline approaches without submitted selections
- [ ] Review records where multiple reviewers provide conflicting decisions
- [ ] Review records where a retouched revision or product choice changes the selected output

- [ ] Check for using filenames that change between proof and final
- [ ] Check for starting retouching from an unsubmitted favorites list
- [ ] Check for combining conflicting feedback from several approvers
- [ ] Check for replacing a proof without preserving the prior decision

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Selection cycle time, Revision approval time, Post-approval change rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
