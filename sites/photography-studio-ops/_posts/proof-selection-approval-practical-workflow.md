Clients favorite images, comment in email, request retouching, and revise selections without one authoritative decision set tied to exact image versions. For independent photography studios and small commercial photo teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every client selection and approval identifies the exact images, revision, intended output, approver, and final decision**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open photo proof decision needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every client selection and approval identifies the exact images, revision, intended output, approver, and final decision
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep the studio CRM, contract, gallery, asset, and delivery platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Publish the controlled proof set

Record **Client, shoot, and gallery** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can collect image-specific selections and comments, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Collect image-specific selections and comments

Record **Proof-set version** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can confirm product, usage, and retouching scope, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Confirm product, usage, and retouching scope

Record **Image identifier** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can return revisions for approval when required, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Return revisions for approval when required

Record **Selection status and intended output** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can freeze approved finals and release production, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Freeze approved finals and release production

Record **Client comment** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a proof deadline approaches without submitted selections
- multiple reviewers provide conflicting decisions
- a retouched revision or product choice changes the selected output

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
