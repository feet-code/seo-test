A booked shoot can still lack signed scope, call times, location access, subject list, permits, props, equipment, weather plan, or client confirmation. The recurring failures are usually process-design problems rather than motivation problems. For independent photography studios and small commercial photo teams, these are the mistakes worth finding before buying or building software.


### 1. Treating a calendar booking as client confirmation

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Date, call time, and duration** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Completion requires recorded evidence that every scheduled shoot reaches a verified go, revised, or postponed decision with people, place, scope, and production resources aligned When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 2. Building the equipment list from an older shot list

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Location and access contact** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 3. Ignoring location access outside business hours

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Subject or shot list** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Keep the studio CRM, contract, gallery, asset, and delivery platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 4. Sending a call sheet before the contingency decision

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Crew and responsibilities** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Every open shoot readiness item needs one owner and a next review time When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

## Audit five recent records

Pick five completed or abandoned examples and ask:

- Can we reconstruct client, shoot, and contract without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct date, call time, and duration without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct location and access contact without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct subject or shot list without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct crew and responsibilities without asking the original owner?

If the answer is no, improve the capture point rather than adding a later reporting step. Reports cannot recover decisions that were never recorded.

## Use mistakes as software requirements

Turn every frequent failure into a testable requirement. “Better visibility” is vague; “show every record with no owner or next date” can be tested. “More automation” is vague; “stop reminders after the completion condition is recorded” can be tested.
