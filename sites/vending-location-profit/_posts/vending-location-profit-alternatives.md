Revenue reports hide the time and route cost required to keep each location stocked. There are several valid ways to manage vending machine location profitability and route optimization. The right choice depends on volume, exception rate, ownership, and how much coordination crosses systems. Start with the smallest approach that can reliably produce this outcome: **Shows true location contribution and recommends product, visit-frequency, commission, or removal decisions**.

## Option comparison

### Owner-maintained spreadsheet

- **Best when:** volume is low, one person owns the decision, and assumptions change often
- **Main limitation:** manual joins and inconsistent follow-through become hard to audit

### Existing industry-system reports

- **Best when:** the source system already contains costs, outcomes, and decision rules
- **Main limitation:** reports often stop at activity or revenue instead of the full money outcome

### General BI tool or analyst

- **Best when:** the buyer has data skills and needs several adjacent analyses
- **Main limitation:** analysis alone may not put an approved action back into the operating cycle

## Choose the manual option when

One owner can see the entire queue, the workflow changes often, and missed handoffs are rare. Document the process anyway so growth does not depend on that person's memory.

## Choose a general platform when

The team already uses it consistently and the workflow shares records with adjacent work. Confirm that statuses, reminders, and permissions can be configured without creating a second shadow spreadsheet.

## Choose a focused tool when

- a new quote, order, job, customer, asset, or contract
- a material change in cost, price, utilization, or timing
- a renewal, repricing, planning, or exception-review cycle

A focused tool should reduce those specific coordination costs. If it merely presents the same data in a prettier view, the migration is unlikely to pay off.

## Run a two-week experiment

Select ten live records. Implement source record and reporting period, customer, job, asset, location, or contract identifier, revenue or avoided-loss amount, variable cost and allocated capacity cost, volume, timing, utilization, or risk inputs, recommended action, owner, confidence, and review date, and follow this sequence: Collect the minimum source records needed to evaluate vending machine location profitability and route optimization. → Normalize revenue, cost, volume, timing, and exception inputs before calculation. → Calculate the margin result and expose the assumptions behind it. → Let the route owner or operations manager approve an action and compare the eventual result with the forecast.. Track incremental gross profit or avoided loss, financial realization rate, forecast error, decision cycle time. At the end, review every exception and ask whether the tool made the next action clearer.

## Preserve reversibility

Export the trial data, document status definitions, and keep the previous process available until the new one completes a full cycle. A good decision is not just about features; it is about whether the team can adopt, operate, and leave the system without losing its history.

Record the decision date and the conditions that would justify reviewing the choice again.
