Examples make land survey deliverable quality review easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small land-surveying firms coordinating field crews and office deliverables can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A plat references an older field file

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Client project parcel and deliverable type, Field dataset date crew and version, Calculations control and adjustment files, then move it through freeze the candidate field and office inputs and run calculation drafting and completeness checks. If field and drafting work is ready for review, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. ## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open survey deliverable needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every survey deliverable is traceable to current field and office inputs, passes the firm's required professional review, and is delivered as a controlled version?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the survey proposal, project, parcel, crew, field-data, cad, review, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
