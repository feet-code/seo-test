Examples make land survey field crew readiness easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small land-surveying firms coordinating field crews and office deliverables can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A boundary job lacks the referenced plat

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Client project parcel and task, Scope deliverable and due date, Deeds plats control and prior survey files, then move it through review project scope and requested deliverable and assemble parcel research control and prior records. If a field task enters the schedule, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A gate contact is unavailable on survey morning

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Scope deliverable and due date, Deeds plats control and prior survey files, Coordinate system data files and version, then move it through review project scope and requested deliverable and assemble parcel research control and prior records. If scope access research or file version changes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A revised control file does not reach the data collector

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Deeds plats control and prior survey files, Coordinate system data files and version, Access permission contact and timing, then move it through review project scope and requested deliverable and assemble parcel research control and prior records. If the crew reports a missing or conflicting prerequisite, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open field assignment needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every survey field assignment has current office research, site permissions, technical files, equipment, crew capability, and explicit release?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the survey proposal, project, parcel, crew, field-data, cad, review, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
