A checklist for land survey field crew readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small land-surveying firms coordinating field crews and office deliverables and centers on one result: **every survey field assignment has current office research, site permissions, technical files, equipment, crew capability, and explicit release**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client project parcel and task
- Confirm Scope deliverable and due date
- Confirm Deeds plats control and prior survey files
- Confirm Coordinate system data files and version

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Review project scope and requested deliverable
- Update Assemble parcel research control and prior records
- Update Confirm access hazards schedule and client contact
- Update Match crew equipment and digital files
- Update Release the versioned field packet and acknowledge receipt

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Access permission contact and timing
- Verify Hazards utility traffic and site conditions
- Verify Crew roles equipment and calibration
- Verify Office reviewer release and crew acknowledgment

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a field task enters the schedule
- [ ] Review records where scope access research or file version changes
- [ ] Review records where the crew reports a missing or conflicting prerequisite

- [ ] Check for sending field data without coordinate-system context
- [ ] Check for assuming public visibility means property access permission
- [ ] Check for using a prior parcel file without revision review
- [ ] Check for changing scope after dispatch without version acknowledgment

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-by-dispatch rate, Field-stop rate, Wrong-version rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
