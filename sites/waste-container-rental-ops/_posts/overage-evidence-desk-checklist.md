A checklist for dumpster contamination and overage evidence tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small roll-off dumpster and commercial waste-container rental companies and centers on one result: **every exception charge is linked to the contract rule, timestamped field or scale evidence, reviewer decision, and customer notice before invoicing**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer order site and container
- Confirm Exception type and detected time
- Confirm Contract rule price and threshold
- Confirm Driver photos notes and location

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the exception from driver or scale evidence
- Update Match it to order and contract rule
- Update Validate amount photos ticket and timing
- Update Review charge waive or correction decision
- Update Notify the customer and release billing

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Scale ticket weight and facility
- Verify Calculation tax and proposed charge
- Verify Reviewer decision and rationale
- Verify Customer notice dispute and invoice status

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a driver or facility identifies a billable exception
- [ ] Review records where required evidence or contract rule is missing
- [ ] Review records where the customer disputes the proposed charge

- [ ] Check for photographing contamination after unloading
- [ ] Check for applying a charge from a generic price list instead of the contract
- [ ] Check for editing ticket weight to fit a threshold
- [ ] Check for sending an invoice before an internal exception review

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Evidence-complete rate, Decision cycle time, Dispute rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
