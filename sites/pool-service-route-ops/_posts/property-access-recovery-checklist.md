A checklist for pool service gate and property access tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent pool maintenance and repair companies running recurring routes and centers on one result: **every access failure is resolved into verified future instructions, an accountable contact, and an explicit billing or reschedule outcome**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and pool
- Confirm Stop time and technician
- Confirm Access method attempted
- Confirm Failure reason and photo if appropriate

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Record the failed or risky access attempt
- Update Contact the approved property person
- Update Validate new instructions without exposing excess detail
- Update Update the route system of record
- Update Recover service and verify the next visit path

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Approved contact and response
- Verify New instruction and effective window
- Verify Reservice billing or skip decision
- Verify Verification and next-stop note

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a technician cannot reach the service area
- [ ] Review records where the customer changes gate pet or tenant arrangements
- [ ] Review records where a previously corrected property fails again

- [ ] Check for saving sensitive access details in an unrestricted note
- [ ] Check for retrying the same instruction without confirmation
- [ ] Check for blaming the technician before comparing the routed record
- [ ] Check for closing once a code is received instead of tested

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Access-failure rate, Same-week recovery rate, Repeat-access failure. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
