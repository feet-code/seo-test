Examples make pool service repair estimate approval tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent pool maintenance and repair companies running recurring routes can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A pump leak needs model and serial confirmation

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer pool and service stop, Equipment type model and serial, Finding symptoms and photos, then move it through open a repair finding from the service stop and confirm equipment identity and diagnosis evidence. If a technician records a repairable finding, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A customer compares repair and replacement options

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Equipment type model and serial, Finding symptoms and photos, Safety or service impact, then move it through open a repair finding from the service stop and confirm equipment identity and diagnosis evidence. If a customer asks a scope or price question, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: An approved heater part moves to backorder

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Finding symptoms and photos, Safety or service impact, Repair options and assumptions, then move it through open a repair finding from the service stop and confirm equipment identity and diagnosis evidence. If price parts or operating impact changes before decision, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open repair proposal needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every repair finding becomes a complete customer decision with current scope, price, parts expectation, and an owned next action?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the pool-service route, customer, reading, chemical, work-order, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
