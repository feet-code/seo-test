A checklist for pool service repair estimate approval tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent pool maintenance and repair companies running recurring routes and centers on one result: **every repair finding becomes a complete customer decision with current scope, price, parts expectation, and an owned next action**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer pool and service stop
- Confirm Equipment type model and serial
- Confirm Finding symptoms and photos
- Confirm Safety or service impact

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open a repair finding from the service stop
- Update Confirm equipment identity and diagnosis evidence
- Update Build options scope and price
- Update Collect customer decision and questions
- Update Schedule approved work or close the declined option

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Repair options and assumptions
- Verify Price tax and validity date
- Verify Customer response and authorization evidence
- Verify Parts status schedule or declined reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a technician records a repairable finding
- [ ] Review records where a customer asks a scope or price question
- [ ] Review records where price parts or operating impact changes before decision

- [ ] Check for quoting from a generic equipment description
- [ ] Check for treating quote delivery as customer review
- [ ] Check for ordering nonreturnable parts before authorization
- [ ] Check for keeping expired pricing active after supplier cost changes

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Decision cycle time, Complete-first-proposal rate, Approved-to-scheduled time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
