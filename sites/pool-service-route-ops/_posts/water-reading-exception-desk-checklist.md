A checklist for pool service water chemistry exception tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent pool maintenance and repair companies running recurring routes and centers on one result: **every material pool-reading exception has verified input, approved response, owner, customer notice, and a scheduled recheck**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer pool and route stop
- Confirm Reading time method and technician
- Confirm Measured values and expected range
- Confirm Recent treatment and weather context

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture readings and pool conditions
- Update Validate the measurement and recent history
- Update Select the approved response path
- Update Notify the customer and assign follow-up
- Update Recheck the condition and document closure

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Observed equipment or water condition
- Verify Approved action and chemical amount
- Verify Customer restriction or notice
- Verify Recheck result owner and time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a recorded value crosses the company's action boundary
- [ ] Review records where readings conflict with observed pool condition or recent history
- [ ] Review records where a recheck remains out of range

- [ ] Check for acting on a likely input error without retesting
- [ ] Check for making treatment recommendations outside approved company rules
- [ ] Check for sending a warning without a recheck owner
- [ ] Check for closing after chemical addition rather than verified result

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Verified-exception cycle, First-recheck resolution, Unowned exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
