A checklist for makerspace machine downtime and maintenance tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for community makerspaces, fabrication labs, and shared technical workshops and centers on one result: **every equipment incident immediately blocks affected access and bookings, transfers with named repair ownership, and restores only after the required review and test**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Space equipment and asset ID
- Confirm Reported time user and symptoms
- Confirm Safety impact and immediate containment
- Confirm Physical tag access and booking state

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture fault asset and user impact
- Update Apply physical and digital lockout
- Update Assign qualified diagnosis or repair
- Update Communicate booking alternatives and status
- Update Complete required test review and controlled restoration

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Diagnostics repair owner and part
- Verify Affected reservations and member notice
- Verify Test procedure result and reviewer
- Verify Restored capability time and follow-up

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a user or inspection reports a machine fault
- [ ] Review records where repair eta changes affected reservations
- [ ] Review records where completed work reaches required return review

- [ ] Check for hanging a sign but leaving remote booking open
- [ ] Check for allowing informal troubleshooting during lockout
- [ ] Check for letting a volunteer self-approve return to service
- [ ] Check for restoring one feature while advertising full capability

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Digital-containment time, Verified downtime, Post-restore recurrence. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
