A checklist for septic disposal ticket and pump record reconciliation should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small septic pumping, inspection, and liquid-waste service companies and centers on one result: **every pumped load reconciles to source jobs, truck custody, accepted disposal evidence, fees, and billable service records**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Truck driver and load
- Confirm Source jobs properties and pump records
- Confirm Volume by job and total
- Confirm Departure and facility arrival times

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the load from completed pump records
- Update Link source jobs and measured volumes
- Update Record transport and disposal event
- Update Compare accepted volume fees and evidence
- Update Resolve variance and release accounting

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Disposal facility and ticket number
- Verify Accepted volume fee and ticket image
- Verify Variance reason and reviewer
- Verify Billing and accounting release

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a truck completes a disposal event
- [ ] Review records where ticket volume or fee differs from linked pump records
- [ ] Review records where a source job or disposal ticket remains unmatched at day close

- [ ] Check for entering one ticket against only the last job
- [ ] Check for ignoring measurement-basis differences
- [ ] Check for reusing a ticket image for multiple loads
- [ ] Check for closing a variance by editing source volume without explanation

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Matched-load rate, Ticket receipt time, Volume variance. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
