A checklist for septic pumping property access readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small septic pumping, inspection, and liquid-waste service companies and centers on one result: **every dispatched septic job has a usable tank location, access plan, service scope, and customer responsibility confirmed before truck commitment**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and contact
- Confirm Service type and scheduled window
- Confirm Tank count type and location evidence
- Confirm Lid exposure and customer preparation

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Load the property and service history
- Update Confirm tank access and customer preparation
- Update Review truck hose parking and site constraints
- Update Resolve exceptions before dispatch
- Update Release the job packet and arrival notice

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Gate access pets and occupant status
- Verify Truck parking hose distance and terrain
- Verify Prior service and known risks
- Verify Reviewer release and customer confirmation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a septic job enters tomorrow's route
- [ ] Review records where the customer cannot confirm a required access detail
- [ ] Review records where the driver reports a readiness mismatch

- [ ] Check for using a billing address as the service location
- [ ] Check for accepting tank location unknown as ready
- [ ] Check for ignoring hose distance when assigning the truck
- [ ] Check for treating an automated reminder as customer confirmation

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-before-dispatch rate, Onsite access failure rate, Unplanned setup time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
