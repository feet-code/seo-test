A checklist for travel supplier confirmation tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent travel advisors and boutique travel agencies and centers on one result: **every itinerary component has a supplier confirmation, matching terms, and an owner for unresolved differences**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Trip, traveler, and component
- Confirm Supplier and booking channel
- Confirm Service dates and travelers
- Confirm Booked product and special request

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the booked component and expected confirmation
- Update Request or import supplier confirmation
- Update Compare dates, travelers, service, price, and terms
- Update Resolve missing or conflicting details
- Update Publish the confirmed component to the itinerary

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Price, currency, and payment terms
- Verify Supplier confirmation number and time
- Verify Mismatch and owner
- Verify Verified itinerary version

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a booking lacks confirmation by its expected time
- [ ] Review records where supplier terms differ from the sold itinerary
- [ ] Review records where a trip amendment or cancellation changes the component

- [ ] Check for counting payment as supplier confirmation
- [ ] Check for copying a confirmation number without checking dates
- [ ] Check for updating the itinerary but not the supplier record
- [ ] Check for sending repeated requests after a component is canceled

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Confirmation lead time, First-match rate, Unconfirmed departure exposure. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
