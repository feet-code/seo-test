A checklist for candidate follow-up tracking for recruiting agencies should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms and centers on one result: **every active candidate receives the promised next update or a documented closed reason**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Candidate and role
- Confirm Last meaningful contact
- Confirm Promised update
- Confirm Next-contact date

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the communication promise
- Update Set the next-contact date
- Update Send the contextual update
- Update Record the response
- Update Close or schedule the next step

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Channel
- Verify Owner
- Verify Response outcome
- Verify Closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a promised update date passes without a logged message
- [ ] Review records where a candidate replies with a question or changed availability
- [ ] Review records where a role is paused, filled, or reassigned

- [ ] Check for recording messages without a next-contact date
- [ ] Check for sending generic check-ins with no useful update
- [ ] Check for continuing reminders after a candidate withdraws
- [ ] Check for keeping ownership implicit when a role changes hands

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Promise kept rate, Open follow-up age, Response outcome mix. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
