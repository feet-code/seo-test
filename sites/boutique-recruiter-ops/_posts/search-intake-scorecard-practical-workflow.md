Recruiters start sourcing with vague or conflicting requirements, then lose time recalibrating after weak candidate submissions. For independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **the recruiter and client can evaluate the same candidate evidence against the same explicit tradeoffs**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Criteria must describe observable evidence
- Tradeoffs belong in the brief before sourcing volume increases
- Every post-launch change needs an owner and reason
- The client approves the scorecard, not just the job description

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Capture the business outcome

Record **Role outcome** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can separate must-haves from preferences, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Separate must-haves from preferences

Record **Must-have criteria** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can define evidence and disqualifiers, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Define evidence and disqualifiers

Record **Flexible criteria** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can calibrate on sample profiles, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Calibrate on sample profiles

Record **Acceptable evidence** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can approve the search brief, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Approve the search brief

Record **Disqualifiers** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- two sample profiles receive conflicting evaluations
- the client adds a new requirement after sourcing starts
- submitted candidates wait beyond the agreed feedback window

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
