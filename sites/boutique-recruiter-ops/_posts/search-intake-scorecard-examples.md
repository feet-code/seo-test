Examples make recruiting search intake and candidate calibration scorecards easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A founder says they need a strategic operator but screens only for a specific title

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Role outcome, Must-have criteria, Flexible criteria, then move it through capture the business outcome and separate must-haves from preferences. If two sample profiles receive conflicting evaluations, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Two interviewers disagree about whether industry experience is mandatory

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Must-have criteria, Flexible criteria, Acceptable evidence, then move it through capture the business outcome and separate must-haves from preferences. If the client adds a new requirement after sourcing starts, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: The compensation ceiling excludes every approved sample profile

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Flexible criteria, Acceptable evidence, Disqualifiers, then move it through capture the business outcome and separate must-haves from preferences. If submitted candidates wait beyond the agreed feedback window, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make criteria must describe observable evidence?
- Did the record make tradeoffs belong in the brief before sourcing volume increases?
- Did the record make every post-launch change needs an owner and reason?
- Did the record make the client approves the scorecard, not just the job description?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
