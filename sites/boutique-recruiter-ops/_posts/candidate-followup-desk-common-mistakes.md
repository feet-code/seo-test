Candidate communication is spread across email, LinkedIn, calendars, and ATS notes, so promised updates are easy to miss. The recurring failures are usually process-design problems rather than motivation problems. For independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms, these are the mistakes worth finding before buying or building software.


### 1. Recording messages without a next-contact date

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Last meaningful contact** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Every message should communicate a real status or next decision When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 2. Sending generic check-ins with no useful update

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Promised update** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: The ATS remains the candidate system of record When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 3. Continuing reminders after a candidate withdraws

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Next-contact date** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Sensitive candidate details should not be copied into unnecessary tools When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 4. Keeping ownership implicit when a role changes hands

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Channel** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: A reminder must stop when the candidate or role closes When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

## Audit five recent records

Pick five completed or abandoned examples and ask:

- Can we reconstruct candidate and role without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct last meaningful contact without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct promised update without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct next-contact date without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct channel without asking the original owner?

If the answer is no, improve the capture point rather than adding a later reporting step. Reports cannot recover decisions that were never recorded.

## Use mistakes as software requirements

Turn every frequent failure into a testable requirement. “Better visibility” is vague; “show every record with no owner or next date” can be tested. “More automation” is vague; “stop reminders after the completion condition is recorded” can be tested.
