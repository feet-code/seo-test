A checklist for recruiting search intake and candidate calibration scorecards should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms and centers on one result: **the recruiter and client can evaluate the same candidate evidence against the same explicit tradeoffs**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Role outcome
- Confirm Must-have criteria
- Confirm Flexible criteria
- Confirm Acceptable evidence

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the business outcome
- Update Separate must-haves from preferences
- Update Define evidence and disqualifiers
- Update Calibrate on sample profiles
- Update Approve the search brief

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Disqualifiers
- Verify Compensation boundary
- Verify Interview owners
- Verify Calibration notes

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where two sample profiles receive conflicting evaluations
- [ ] Review records where the client adds a new requirement after sourcing starts
- [ ] Review records where submitted candidates wait beyond the agreed feedback window

- [ ] Check for treating every preference as mandatory
- [ ] Check for using adjectives that cannot be evaluated
- [ ] Check for skipping compensation and location boundaries
- [ ] Check for changing criteria after submissions without logging the decision

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Calibration change count, Submission acceptance rate, Decision turnaround. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
