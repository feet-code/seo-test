The most useful recruiting search intake and candidate calibration scorecards template is a small operating record. It should answer what is happening, who owns it, what evidence exists, and when the next decision occurs. This structure works in a spreadsheet, database, or focused application.

## Recommended record fields

| Field | Why it exists | Update point |

## Suggested statuses

Use workflow statuses that describe reality: **Capture The Business Outcome → Separate Must Haves From Preferences → Define Evidence And Disqualifiers → Calibrate On Sample Profiles → Approve The Search Brief**. Add **Waiting** only when you also capture a waiting reason and review date. Add **Closed—Not Completed** when an item legitimately ends without the desired outcome.

## Follow-up rules

- When two sample profiles receive conflicting evaluations, assign a next action and review date.
- When the client adds a new requirement after sourcing starts, assign a next action and review date.
- When submitted candidates wait beyond the agreed feedback window, assign a next action and review date.

Avoid reminders with no stop condition. A rule should say when it starts, who receives it, what counts as a response, and when a person should take over.

## Example records

- A founder says they need a strategic operator but screens only for a specific title
- Two interviewers disagree about whether industry experience is mandatory
- The compensation ceiling excludes every approved sample profile

For each example, write the current status, next action, owner, and supporting evidence. This makes the template testable with real work rather than idealized sample data.

## Quality-control rules

- Criteria must describe observable evidence
- Tradeoffs belong in the brief before sourcing volume increases
- Every post-launch change needs an owner and reason
- The client approves the scorecard, not just the job description

Before adding automation, run the template manually for a week. Remove ambiguous fields and confirm that two different users classify the same situation the same way. Consistency matters more than having a long form.
