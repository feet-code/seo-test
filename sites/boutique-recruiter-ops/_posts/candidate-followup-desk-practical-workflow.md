Candidate communication is spread across email, LinkedIn, calendars, and ATS notes, so promised updates are easy to miss. For independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every active candidate receives the promised next update or a documented closed reason**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- A reminder must stop when the candidate or role closes
- Every message should communicate a real status or next decision
- The ATS remains the candidate system of record
- Sensitive candidate details should not be copied into unnecessary tools

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Capture the communication promise

Record **Candidate and role** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can set the next-contact date, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Set the next-contact date

Record **Last meaningful contact** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can send the contextual update, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Send the contextual update

Record **Promised update** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can record the response, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Record the response

Record **Next-contact date** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close or schedule the next step, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Close or schedule the next step

Record **Channel** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a promised update date passes without a logged message
- a candidate replies with a question or changed availability
- a role is paused, filled, or reassigned

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
