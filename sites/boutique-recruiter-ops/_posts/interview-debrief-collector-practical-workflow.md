Interview feedback arrives late, lacks evidence, or is shaped by the loudest interviewer, delaying client decisions and weakening candidate communication. For independent recruiters and boutique recruiting firms, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **the client reaches an evidence-based candidate decision while the interview is still fresh**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Independent feedback is captured before group discussion
- Evidence is separated from conclusions
- Missing feedback has a cutoff and escalation owner
- The final decision includes the candidate communication owner

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Assign evaluation areas

Record **Candidate and stage** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can collect independent feedback, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Collect independent feedback

Record **Interviewer** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can flag missing evidence, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Flag missing evidence

Record **Evaluation area** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can run the debrief, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Run the debrief

Record **Evidence observed** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can record the decision and candidate update, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Record the decision and candidate update

Record **Concern** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- an interviewer has not submitted before the debrief cutoff
- two evaluations conflict on the same criterion
- a concern has no supporting example or follow-up question

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
