A checklist for sports league rainout rescheduling should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for community sports leagues and small tournament operators and centers on one result: **every weather-affected game is canceled, relocated, or rescheduled with all dependent assignments and communications reconciled**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm League, division, and game
- Confirm Field and original time
- Confirm Weather decision source and time
- Confirm Teams and contacts

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the weather exception against affected games
- Update Confirm field decision and cancellation authority
- Update Find viable date, field, and team availability
- Update Reassign officials and facility resources
- Update Publish and verify the replacement schedule

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Candidate field and date
- Verify Official and facility assignments
- Verify Published replacement version
- Verify Acknowledgments and unresolved conflicts

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a field or weather authority changes playability
- [ ] Review records where a candidate replacement conflicts with a team, field, or official
- [ ] Review records where the published replacement changes again

- [ ] Check for announcing a cancellation before the authorized field decision
- [ ] Check for moving a game without checking official availability
- [ ] Check for creating a replacement but leaving the original active
- [ ] Check for sending a broad message without identifying affected games

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Reschedule cycle time, First-publish conflict rate, Acknowledgment coverage. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
