A checklist for sports official assignment acceptance tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for community sports leagues and small tournament operators and centers on one result: **every game has the required qualified officials who explicitly accept and receive the current assignment details**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm League, game, field, and time
- Confirm Official role and qualification
- Confirm Candidate availability and conflict
- Confirm Offer sent and response deadline

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create required official slots from the game schedule
- Update Match qualification, availability, and conflicts
- Update Offer the assignment with response deadline
- Update Confirm acceptance or route replacement
- Update Deliver final game details and reconcile payment status

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Accepted official
- Verify Assignment version
- Verify Game-detail acknowledgment
- Verify Completion and payment status

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an official slot opens or an offer expires
- [ ] Review records where an accepted official reports a conflict or callout
- [ ] Review records where game date, field, time, or role changes

- [ ] Check for counting message delivery as acceptance
- [ ] Check for assigning an official with a team conflict
- [ ] Check for changing game details without renewing acknowledgment
- [ ] Check for paying from a separate list that still shows the prior official

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Accepted-by-deadline rate, Reassignment rate, Uncovered game exposure. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
