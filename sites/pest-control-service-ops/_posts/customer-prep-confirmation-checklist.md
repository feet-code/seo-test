A checklist for pest control service preparation confirmation should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent pest control companies and small recurring-service teams and centers on one result: **every treatment starts with the required customer preparation confirmed or a documented service decision before technician arrival**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and service
- Confirm Treatment type and target area
- Confirm Preparation checklist version
- Confirm Required-by and visit window

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create requirements from service type and property
- Update Send plain-language preparation instructions
- Update Collect customer confirmation and questions
- Update Review exceptions before routing
- Update Release, adjust, or reschedule the visit

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Delivery channel and evidence
- Verify Customer response and questions
- Verify Office decision and technician note
- Verify Released or rescheduled outcome

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a preparation-required service is booked
- [ ] Review records where the customer reports an unmet requirement
- [ ] Review records where the visit time or treatment scope changes

- [ ] Check for treating a delivered message as confirmation
- [ ] Check for using one checklist for every treatment type
- [ ] Check for sending reminders after cancellation
- [ ] Check for leaving the technician to negotiate a material exception onsite

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-before-route rate, Onsite preparation failure rate, Avoided drive rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
