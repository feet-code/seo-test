Examples make pest control callback and retreatment tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent pest control companies and small recurring-service teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: Ant activity returns in a covered area

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer property and prior service, Pest or condition reported, Callback time and channel, then move it through register the callback against prior service and collect current observations and evidence. If a customer reports activity after service, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A new pest is reported after a termite service

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Pest or condition reported, Callback time and channel, Photos observations and affected areas, then move it through register the callback against prior service and collect current observations and evidence. If coverage or urgency cannot be determined from intake, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A retreatment is complete but the customer still observes activity

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Callback time and channel, Photos observations and affected areas, Agreement coverage and decision, then move it through register the callback against prior service and collect current observations and evidence. If a completed callback produces another report, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open callback request needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every callback is classified against the service agreement, routed with prior evidence, and closed only after the promised resolution is verified?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the pest-control crm, route, service-history, chemical-use, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
