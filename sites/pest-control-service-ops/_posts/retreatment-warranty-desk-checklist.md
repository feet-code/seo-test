A checklist for pest control callback and retreatment tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent pest control companies and small recurring-service teams and centers on one result: **every callback is classified against the service agreement, routed with prior evidence, and closed only after the promised resolution is verified**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer property and prior service
- Confirm Pest or condition reported
- Confirm Callback time and channel
- Confirm Photos observations and affected areas

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the callback against prior service
- Update Collect current observations and evidence
- Update Review coverage and urgency
- Update Dispatch the appropriate response
- Update Verify result and close or escalate

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Agreement coverage and decision
- Verify Assigned technician and visit window
- Verify New findings and treatment action
- Verify Customer confirmation and closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a customer reports activity after service
- [ ] Review records where coverage or urgency cannot be determined from intake
- [ ] Review records where a completed callback produces another report

- [ ] Check for opening a new job with no link to prior treatment
- [ ] Check for promising coverage before checking agreement terms
- [ ] Check for sending a technician without the customer's new observations
- [ ] Check for closing when the retreatment is scheduled

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Eligibility decision time, Repeat callback rate, Callback resolution time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
