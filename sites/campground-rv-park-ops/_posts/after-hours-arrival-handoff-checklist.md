A checklist for campground late arrival check in coordination should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent campgrounds, RV parks, and small outdoor lodging properties and centers on one result: **every confirmed after-hours guest receives a current, secure arrival path tied to a ready site and a next-morning verification**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Guest reservation and contact
- Confirm Expected arrival and rig or lodging type
- Confirm Assigned site and readiness state
- Confirm Balance agreement and policy status

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Identify arrivals outside staffed hours
- Update Verify reservation payment agreement and site
- Update Prepare secure property-specific instructions
- Update Confirm delivery and guest understanding
- Update Review arrival outcome at the next staffed handoff

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Gate key lockbox or entry method
- Verify Route directions and site constraints
- Verify Instruction delivery and confirmation
- Verify Arrival evidence exception and morning owner

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a reservation expects arrival after office hours
- [ ] Review records where site assignment access or balance changes after instructions
- [ ] Review records where the guest does not confirm or reports an arrival problem

- [ ] Check for publishing sensitive access details in a public message
- [ ] Check for sending instructions before the site is released
- [ ] Check for using generic directions for oversized rigs
- [ ] Check for closing the handoff when the email sends

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Confirmed-before-close rate, Arrival exception rate, Morning reconciliation time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
