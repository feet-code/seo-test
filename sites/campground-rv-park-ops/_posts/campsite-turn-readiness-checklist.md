A checklist for campground campsite turnover readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent campgrounds, RV parks, and small outdoor lodging properties and centers on one result: **every departing site is inspected, serviced, reconciled, and released for the next arrival or held with a visible reason**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Property site and site type
- Confirm Departing guest and checkout time
- Confirm Utility and hookup condition
- Confirm Cleanup grounds and amenity checks

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Confirm departure and possession of the site
- Update Inspect utilities condition and amenities
- Update Assign cleanup or maintenance
- Update Reconcile fees keys and site status
- Update Verify readiness and release the next reservation

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Damage photos and fee decision
- Verify Maintenance tasks owner and ETA
- Verify Next reservation and arrival time
- Verify Inspector release or hold reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a reservation checks out
- [ ] Review records where inspection finds damage cleanup or utility issue
- [ ] Review records where the next arrival approaches while a hold remains open

- [ ] Check for marking vacant before confirming departure
- [ ] Check for releasing the site while a maintenance task is merely assigned
- [ ] Check for inspecting a cabin checklist against an rv site
- [ ] Check for hiding a site without telling reservations why

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Checkout-to-ready time, First-pass readiness, Late-arrival impact. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
