A checklist for campground cancellation waitlist fill tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent campgrounds, RV parks, and small outdoor lodging properties and centers on one result: **every cancellation opportunity is offered to eligible waitlist guests in a fair visible sequence and returns to public inventory at a defined cutoff**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Property site dates and site type
- Confirm Canceled reservation and release time
- Confirm Waitlist request date and guest
- Confirm Rig fit occupancy and preferences

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open vacancy from the canceled reservation
- Update Filter eligible waitlist requests by fit
- Update Offer with a clear response deadline
- Update Confirm booking payment and removed requests
- Update Release unclaimed inventory and preserve the history

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Offer order channel and sent time
- Verify Response deadline and guest response
- Verify Payment booking and removed conflicts
- Verify Public release or filled outcome

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a cancellation reopens a constrained site
- [ ] Review records where an offered guest declines or misses the deadline
- [ ] Review records where a waitlist guest's dates or rig details change

- [ ] Check for offering a site to a rig that does not fit
- [ ] Check for contacting several guests without an allocation rule
- [ ] Check for holding inventory indefinitely for no response
- [ ] Check for leaving a filled guest on overlapping waitlists

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Vacancy fill rate, Offer response time, Public-release delay. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
