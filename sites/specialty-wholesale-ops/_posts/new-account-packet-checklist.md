A checklist for wholesale customer onboarding and new account setup checklists should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small specialty wholesalers and B2B distributors and centers on one result: **a new account reaches ready-to-order status with every required operational field and approval complete**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Legal business name
- Confirm Billing and ship-to contacts
- Confirm Ordering contact
- Confirm Tax or resale document status
- Confirm Payment terms decision

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Select the account path
- Update Request customer information and documents
- Update Validate and approve terms
- Update Configure ordering and fulfillment
- Update Confirm ready-to-order

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Price list
- Verify Shipping method
- Verify Minimums
- Verify Internal owner
- Verify Ready date

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a required document is missing or rejected
- [ ] Review records where requested terms require internal review
- [ ] Review records where the first-order path is not configured by the target ready date

- [ ] Check for using one packet for cash and terms accounts
- [ ] Check for collecting sensitive documents through informal email
- [ ] Check for marking complete before price and shipping rules are configured
- [ ] Check for failing to tell the buyer how to place the first order

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Onboarding cycle time, First-order correction rate, Missing-item touch count. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
