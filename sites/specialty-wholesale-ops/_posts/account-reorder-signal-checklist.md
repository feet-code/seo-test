A checklist for wholesale customer reorder reminders and account follow-up should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small specialty wholesalers and B2B distributors and centers on one result: **the rep reviews each plausible reorder opportunity at the right time without sending irrelevant automated messages**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Account
- Confirm Item family
- Confirm Prior order date
- Confirm Typical interval
- Confirm Season or event

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Establish the account-item cadence
- Update Create an explainable review date
- Update Check stock and account context
- Update Send or defer contextual outreach
- Update Record the reorder outcome

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Current stock status
- Verify Review date
- Verify Rep owner
- Verify Outreach note
- Verify Outcome

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where the review date arrives with no open order
- [ ] Review records where core items are backordered or substituted
- [ ] Review records where the account reports a changed season, project, or purchasing policy

- [ ] Check for treating past cadence as a guaranteed purchase
- [ ] Check for contacting an account when core items are unavailable
- [ ] Check for ignoring seasonal or project-based purchasing
- [ ] Check for resetting the signal without recording the outcome

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Reviewed-signal conversion, Irrelevant outreach rate, Reorder interval change. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
