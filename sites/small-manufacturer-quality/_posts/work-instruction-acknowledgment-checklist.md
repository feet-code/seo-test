A checklist for controlled work instruction acknowledgment should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small manufacturers and lean quality teams and centers on one result: **every effective instruction revision is distributed to the affected roles and acknowledged with required training before use**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Document ID and revision
- Confirm Effective date and superseded revision
- Confirm Change summary
- Confirm Affected process and station

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Approve and release the controlled revision
- Update Map affected roles, stations, and jobs
- Update Distribute the effective instruction
- Update Capture acknowledgment and qualification
- Update Retire obsolete copies and review exceptions

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Required roles and operators
- Verify Distribution location
- Verify Acknowledgment or qualification evidence
- Verify Obsolete-copy removal and exception

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a revision becomes effective with incomplete acknowledgment
- [ ] Review records where an affected operator or station is added after release
- [ ] Review records where an obsolete copy is found or an operator fails qualification

- [ ] Check for collecting signatures without showing the revision
- [ ] Check for treating read as proof of demonstrated qualification
- [ ] Check for leaving an obsolete print at the workstation
- [ ] Check for assigning every document to every employee

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time acknowledgment, Obsolete-copy exception rate, Qualification completion time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
