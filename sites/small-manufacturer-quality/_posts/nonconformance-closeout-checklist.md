A checklist for manufacturing nonconformance closeout should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small manufacturers and lean quality teams and centers on one result: **every nonconformance is contained, dispositioned by authority, corrected, and closed only after required effectiveness evidence**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Part, lot, job, and quantity
- Confirm Requirement and defect evidence
- Confirm Detection point and date
- Confirm Containment location and scope

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Record the requirement and nonconforming evidence
- Update Contain affected material and identify scope
- Update Approve disposition and responsibility
- Update Complete correction and corrective action
- Update Verify effectiveness and authorize closure

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Disposition and approval
- Verify Cause and corrective action owner
- Verify Due dates and completion evidence
- Verify Effectiveness result and closure authority

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where containment is incomplete for the suspected scope
- [ ] Review records where disposition or corrective action passes its due date
- [ ] Review records where the same defect appears after effectiveness approval

- [ ] Check for closing after rework without addressing required cause review
- [ ] Check for mixing quarantined and released quantities
- [ ] Check for letting the action owner approve their own effectiveness check
- [ ] Check for editing the defect description after disposition without history

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Containment time, Open action age, Recurrence rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
