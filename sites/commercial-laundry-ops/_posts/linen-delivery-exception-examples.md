Examples make commercial laundry delivery exception tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small commercial laundries and linen or uniform rental services can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A hotel receives the wrong towel cart

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer, stop, route, and ticket, Textile item and unit, Planned, loaded, delivered, and returned quantity, then move it through open the exception from route activity and compare contract, load, delivery, and return quantities. If driver or customer reports a delivery difference, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A clinic rejects bags at a locked service entrance

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Textile item and unit, Planned, loaded, delivered, and returned quantity, Exception reason and time, then move it through open the exception from route activity and compare contract, load, delivery, and return quantities. If recovery timing threatens customer par, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: An emergency redelivery is complete but the next standing order remains inflated

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Planned, loaded, delivered, and returned quantity, Exception reason and time, Driver and customer evidence, then move it through open the exception from route activity and compare contract, load, delivery, and return quantities. If redelivery, return, credit, or billing state changes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open linen route exception needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every route delivery exception has verified quantities, customer acknowledgment, recovery plan, and corrected inventory and billing records?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the laundry production, textile inventory, route, contract, and billing system as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
