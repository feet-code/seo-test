A checklist for tutoring parent progress reporting workflow should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent tutoring centers and multi-tutor education businesses and centers on one result: **each reporting period produces an approved, evidence-based parent update with clear progress and next focus**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Student and program
- Confirm Reporting period
- Confirm Goal or skill area
- Confirm Session evidence and observation

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Define the reporting period and required evidence
- Update Collect structured tutor session notes
- Update Flag missing or unclear observations
- Update Review and publish the parent update
- Update Record questions and next-period focus

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Tutor author and submission time
- Verify Reviewer and approval status
- Verify Published summary and channel
- Verify Parent question and next focus

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a required session note is missing near publication
- [ ] Review records where multiple tutors record conflicting progress
- [ ] Review records where a parent asks a question that needs tutor or director review

- [ ] Check for using attendance alone as evidence of progress
- [ ] Check for copying internal tutor shorthand into a parent message
- [ ] Check for publishing conflicting comments from multiple tutors
- [ ] Check for promising an outcome the recorded sessions do not support

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time report rate, Note completeness, Parent question resolution. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
