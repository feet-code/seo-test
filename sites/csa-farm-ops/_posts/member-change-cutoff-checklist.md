A checklist for CSA skip swap and pickup change tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small community-supported agriculture farms and farm-box programs and centers on one result: **every eligible member change is applied before the correct packing and route cutoff or closed with a clear alternative**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Member and subscription
- Confirm Delivery week and pickup site
- Confirm Request type and original message
- Confirm Request time and cutoff

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the member request and effective week
- Update Apply plan rules and the relevant cutoff
- Update Approve the skip, swap, move, or alternative
- Update Update packing, inventory, payment, and route records
- Update Confirm the final outcome to the member

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Eligibility and credit impact
- Verify Approved box or location change
- Verify Packing and route update evidence
- Verify Member confirmation or closed alternative

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a request arrives near or after its cutoff
- [ ] Review records where a swap or pickup move lacks inventory or capacity
- [ ] Review records where the member record and frozen packing list disagree

- [ ] Check for changing the member profile but not the week's packing list
- [ ] Check for accepting a swap after harvest allocation without checking inventory
- [ ] Check for applying a skip to the wrong delivery week
- [ ] Check for promising a pickup move without confirming site capacity

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Pre-cutoff completion, Packing correction rate, Request type mix. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
