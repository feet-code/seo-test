Turnover tasks live in separate vendor texts and checklists, so one missed dependency can delay listing or move-in readiness. For independent residential property managers and small property teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **the unit reaches a documented ready state with every dependency and exception resolved**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Readiness blockers are distinct from cosmetic preferences
- Dependent work cannot be marked ready out of sequence
- Every task has room-level evidence
- One named person approves the final ready state

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Complete the move-out inspection

Record **Property and unit** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can define repair and cleaning scope, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Define repair and cleaning scope

Record **Target ready date** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can schedule dependent vendors, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Schedule dependent vendors

Record **Inspection findings** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can capture readiness evidence, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Capture readiness evidence

Record **Task dependency** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can approve listing or move-in readiness, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Approve listing or move-in readiness

Record **Vendor and appointment** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a predecessor task slips past its dependent appointment
- a finding changes the approved repair scope
- the target ready date is at risk

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
