A checklist for rental property maintenance request triage should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent residential property managers and small property teams and centers on one result: **every request has enough evidence for a clear priority, owner, tenant update, and verified resolution**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Property and unit
- Confirm Issue description
- Confirm Photo or video
- Confirm Safety or habitability signal

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Capture the request
- Update Assess urgency and missing evidence
- Update Assign an owner or vendor
- Update Coordinate access and updates
- Update Verify completion

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Access instructions
- Verify Priority
- Verify Assigned owner
- Verify Tenant update
- Verify Completion evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where the request mentions water, electrical, security, heat, or another safety signal
- [ ] Review records where required photos or access details are missing
- [ ] Review records where a vendor misses the scheduled window or reports a changed scope

- [ ] Check for assigning a vendor before collecting usable evidence
- [ ] Check for using urgent as a catch-all priority
- [ ] Check for failing to record access constraints
- [ ] Check for closing when the vendor says done without tenant or manager confirmation

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Time to triage, Reopen rate, Tenant update compliance. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
