Examples make rental property maintenance request triage easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent residential property managers and small property teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A tenant texts a blurry photo of water under a sink after business hours

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Property and unit, Issue description, Photo or video, then move it through capture the request and assess urgency and missing evidence. If the request mentions water, electrical, security, heat, or another safety signal, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A vendor cannot enter because access instructions were never confirmed

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Issue description, Photo or video, Safety or habitability signal, then move it through capture the request and assess urgency and missing evidence. If required photos or access details are missing, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A repair is marked complete but the tenant reports the symptom returned

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Photo or video, Safety or habitability signal, Access instructions, then move it through capture the request and assess urgency and missing evidence. If a vendor misses the scheduled window or reports a changed scope, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make local emergency and legal requirements remain outside the tool's automated judgment?
- Did the record make urgency decisions require a reason and reviewer?
- Did the record make tenants receive a clear next-update time?
- Did the record make completion requires evidence, not only a status click?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
