The most useful rental unit turnover checklist and make-ready tracking template is a small operating record. It should answer what is happening, who owns it, what evidence exists, and when the next decision occurs. This structure works in a spreadsheet, database, or focused application.

## Suggested statuses

Use workflow statuses that describe reality: **Complete The Move Out Inspection → Define Repair And Cleaning Scope → Schedule Dependent Vendors → Capture Readiness Evidence → Approve Listing Or Move In Readiness**. Add **Waiting** only when you also capture a waiting reason and review date. Add **Closed—Not Completed** when an item legitimately ends without the desired outcome.

## Follow-up rules

- When a predecessor task slips past its dependent appointment, assign a next action and review date.
- When a finding changes the approved repair scope, assign a next action and review date.
- When the target ready date is at risk, assign a next action and review date.

Avoid reminders with no stop condition. A rule should say when it starts, who receives it, what counts as a response, and when a person should take over.

## Example records

- Floor repair pushes cleaning and photography into the same afternoon
- An inspection reveals an appliance issue requiring owner approval
- The unit looks ready in photos but a smoke-alarm check is undocumented

For each example, write the current status, next action, owner, and supporting evidence. This makes the template testable with real work rather than idealized sample data.

## Quality-control rules

- Readiness blockers are distinct from cosmetic preferences
- Dependent work cannot be marked ready out of sequence
- Every task has room-level evidence
- One named person approves the final ready state

Before adding automation, run the template manually for a week. Remove ambiguous fields and confirm that two different users classify the same situation the same way. Consistency matters more than having a long form.
