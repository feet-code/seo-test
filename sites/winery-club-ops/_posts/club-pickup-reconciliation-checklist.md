A checklist for wine club pickup order tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small wineries running direct-to-consumer wine clubs and pickup programs and centers on one result: **every club pickup order is staged and released accurately, converted or canceled by an approved rule, and reconciled to member and inventory records**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Member club release and order
- Confirm Wine quantities lots and storage location
- Confirm Ready date notices and responses
- Confirm Pickup deadline and extension rule

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Stage and label pickup orders by release
- Update Notify members with deadlines and options
- Update Verify collector order and payment at pickup
- Update Handle partial pickup shipping or extension decisions
- Update Reconcile remaining inventory and close the release

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Authorized collector and identification method
- Verify Partial pickup or shipment conversion
- Verify Payment tax and inventory movements
- Verify Release evidence remaining action and close reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a club pickup release becomes ready
- [ ] Review records where the member requests collector extension partial pickup or shipping
- [ ] Review records where the pickup deadline passes with inventory still staged

- [ ] Check for staging inventory without a unit-level order label
- [ ] Check for releasing to a friend with no member authorization
- [ ] Check for shipping a pickup order by canceling and rebuilding without history
- [ ] Check for returning wine to stock without changing the member order

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Pickup-through-deadline rate, Release dwell time, Reconciliation variance. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
