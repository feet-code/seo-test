A checklist for nonprofit participant follow-up and referral tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small nonprofit direct-service and program teams and centers on one result: **every consented program follow-up reaches a documented next step or closed reason without unnecessary data collection**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Program and participant ID
- Confirm Follow-up purpose
- Confirm Consent boundary
- Confirm Preferred channel
- Confirm Owner

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Record the follow-up commitment
- Update Confirm consent and preferred contact
- Update Assign the action
- Update Complete or coordinate the follow-up
- Update Record the outcome and next review

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Due date
- Verify Referral or action
- Verify Attempt outcome
- Verify Next date
- Verify Closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a promised follow-up date arrives
- [ ] Review records where a referral has no confirmation by its review date
- [ ] Review records where staff ownership or participant contact preference changes

- [ ] Check for recording more personal detail than the workflow needs
- [ ] Check for using message sent as the completion outcome
- [ ] Check for failing to distinguish referral made from referral connected
- [ ] Check for leaving overdue records ownerless after staff changes

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time follow-up rate, Connection rate, Closed-reason completeness. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
