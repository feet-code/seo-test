Follow-up commitments are distributed across staff notes and spreadsheets, making it hard to see which participant is waiting and what consented action comes next. For small nonprofit direct-service and program teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every consented program follow-up reaches a documented next step or closed reason without unnecessary data collection**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Collect only information needed for the action
- Consent and contact preferences control outreach
- Referral sent and referral connected are separate outcomes
- Supervisors can reassign open commitments without exposing unnecessary detail

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Record the follow-up commitment

Record **Program and participant ID** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can confirm consent and preferred contact, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Confirm consent and preferred contact

Record **Follow-up purpose** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can assign the action, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Assign the action

Record **Consent boundary** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can complete or coordinate the follow-up, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Complete or coordinate the follow-up

Record **Preferred channel** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can record the outcome and next review, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Record the outcome and next review

Record **Owner** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a promised follow-up date arrives
- a referral has no confirmation by its review date
- staff ownership or participant contact preference changes

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
