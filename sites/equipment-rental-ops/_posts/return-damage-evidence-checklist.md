A checklist for equipment rental return damage documentation should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent equipment, tool, and event-rental businesses and centers on one result: **every returned asset is inspected against checkout evidence and any damage decision is documented before billing or release**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Contract, customer, and asset
- Confirm Checkout condition and media
- Confirm Return time, location, and inspector
- Confirm Meter, fuel, and consumable readings

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Check in the asset and freeze its availability state
- Update Compare return condition with checkout evidence
- Update Document damage, missing items, and usage
- Update Approve charge, waiver, or internal repair decision
- Update Notify the customer and release or hold the asset

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Damage description and photos
- Verify Missing accessories
- Verify Decision, approver, and estimated cost
- Verify Customer notice and asset disposition

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an asset is returned with condition different from checkout
- [ ] Review records where a required accessory or meter reading is missing
- [ ] Review records where damage affects safety, availability, waiver coverage, or customer billing

- [ ] Check for cleaning or renting the asset before evidence is captured
- [ ] Check for using undated photos with no asset identifier
- [ ] Check for charging the customer before applying waiver or preexisting-condition evidence
- [ ] Check for marking available while a safety-related issue is open

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Inspection cycle time, Evidence-complete rate, Decision revision rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
