A checklist for overdue equipment rental follow-up should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent equipment, tool, and event-rental businesses and centers on one result: **every overdue contract has confirmed asset status, an authorized return or extension plan, and protected downstream reservations**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Contract, customer, and asset
- Confirm Original due time and location
- Confirm Future reservation dependency
- Confirm Contact attempts and responses

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the overdue record at the return cutoff
- Update Verify contract, asset, and contact status
- Update Contact the customer with the required action
- Update Approve extension, recovery, or escalation
- Update Reconcile return, billing, and future availability

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Current asset location and condition
- Verify Extension terms and approver
- Verify Recovery or escalation owner
- Verify Actual return and billing reconciliation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where the contracted return time passes with no check-in
- [ ] Review records where an overdue asset threatens another reservation
- [ ] Review records where the customer requests an extension or cannot confirm asset location

- [ ] Check for extending the contract without checking the next reservation
- [ ] Check for sending reminders after the return is recorded in another location
- [ ] Check for threatening escalation outside the documented policy
- [ ] Check for changing due time without preserving the original commitment

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Overdue resolution time, Reservation conflict exposure, Contact-to-plan rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
