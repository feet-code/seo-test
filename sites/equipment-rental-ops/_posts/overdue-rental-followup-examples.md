Examples make overdue equipment rental follow-up easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent equipment, tool, and event-rental businesses can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A contractor keeps a skid steer into the next reservation

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Contract, customer, and asset, Original due time and location, Future reservation dependency, then move it through open the overdue record at the return cutoff and verify contract, asset, and contact status. If the contracted return time passes with no check-in, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: An event customer returns items to the wrong warehouse

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Original due time and location, Future reservation dependency, Contact attempts and responses, then move it through open the overdue record at the return cutoff and verify contract, asset, and contact status. If an overdue asset threatens another reservation, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A renter requests a weekend extension after the counter closes

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Future reservation dependency, Contact attempts and responses, Current asset location and condition, then move it through open the overdue record at the return cutoff and verify contract, asset, and contact status. If the customer requests an extension or cannot confirm asset location, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open overdue rental needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every overdue contract has confirmed asset status, an authorized return or extension plan, and protected downstream reservations?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep rental contract, asset, billing, and maintenance system as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
