A checklist for influencer product sample tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small direct-to-consumer ecommerce brands and lean operations teams and centers on one result: **every creator sample has documented consent and terms, confirmed delivery, and a closed deliverable or no-obligation outcome**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Creator identity and channel
- Confirm Campaign and product SKU
- Confirm Relationship type and terms
- Confirm Consent and shipping address

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Qualify the creator and record the proposed exchange
- Update Confirm address, consent, and terms
- Update Reserve and ship the sample
- Update Track delivery and contextual follow-up
- Update Record content, rights, return, or closed outcome

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Shipment and delivery evidence
- Verify Deliverable and expected window
- Verify Follow-up owner and status
- Verify Content link, usage rights, or closed reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a sample is unshipped near the campaign window
- [ ] Review records where delivery fails or the creator changes address
- [ ] Review records where a contracted deliverable is due or content rights are unclear

- [ ] Check for treating an unsolicited gift as a guaranteed post
- [ ] Check for keeping addresses in an unnecessarily broad sheet
- [ ] Check for chasing a creator after terms say there is no obligation
- [ ] Check for publishing creator content without recorded usage rights

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Delivery confirmation rate, Agreed deliverable completion, Sample inventory cost. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
