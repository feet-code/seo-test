Examples make influencer product sample tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases small direct-to-consumer ecommerce brands and lean operations teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A creator accepts a no-obligation product gift

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Creator identity and channel, Campaign and product SKU, Relationship type and terms, then move it through qualify the creator and record the proposed exchange and confirm address, consent, and terms. If a sample is unshipped near the campaign window, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A paid partner owes two videos after delivery

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Campaign and product SKU, Relationship type and terms, Consent and shipping address, then move it through qualify the creator and record the proposed exchange and confirm address, consent, and terms. If delivery fails or the creator changes address, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A sample is returned because an address from a DM was incomplete

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Relationship type and terms, Consent and shipping address, Shipment and delivery evidence, then move it through qualify the creator and record the proposed exchange and confirm address, consent, and terms. If a contracted deliverable is due or content rights are unclear, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open creator sample commitment needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every creator sample has documented consent and terms, confirmed delivery, and a closed deliverable or no-obligation outcome?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep ecommerce, order, inventory, and product-information platforms as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
