A checklist for moving inventory change authorization should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent household moving companies and local moving crews and centers on one result: **every material move change is priced, authorized, and published to dispatch and crew before the affected work proceeds**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer, move, and estimate
- Confirm Original and changed inventory
- Confirm Change source and time
- Confirm Origin or destination access change

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Log the requested or observed scope change
- Update Compare it with the approved estimate and inventory
- Update Assess labor, equipment, timing, and price impact
- Update Obtain customer and operations authorization
- Update Publish the effective scope and preserve the prior version

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Labor, vehicle, equipment, and date impact
- Verify Price and valuation impact
- Verify Customer and operations approval
- Verify Effective version and crew acknowledgment

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where the customer adds or removes inventory
- [ ] Review records where crew observes access or packing work outside the estimate
- [ ] Review records where date, address, vehicle, or labor requirements change

- [ ] Check for editing the original estimate without a change record
- [ ] Check for letting the crew negotiate undocumented scope
- [ ] Check for pricing a change without checking vehicle or schedule capacity
- [ ] Check for sending an updated total without identifying what changed

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Pre-work authorization rate, Change review time, Post-move scope disputes. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
