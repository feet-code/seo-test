A checklist for moving company damage claim evidence tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent household moving companies and local moving crews and centers on one result: **every damage or loss claim is acknowledged, completed with required evidence, reviewed, and resolved with a documented decision**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer, shipment, and bill of lading
- Confirm Claim received date and deadline
- Confirm Item and inventory number
- Confirm Damage or loss description

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register the written claim and shipment
- Update Itemize loss or damage against inventory
- Update Collect photos, value, and repair evidence
- Update Review responsibility and authorized remedy
- Update Communicate the decision and record settlement or closure

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Pickup, delivery, and claim photos
- Verify Value, repair estimate, and valuation terms
- Verify Reviewer and decision rationale
- Verify Offer, settlement, denial, or follow-up

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a written loss or damage claim arrives
- [ ] Review records where required item, shipment, photo, or value evidence is missing
- [ ] Review records where inspection, estimate, or customer response changes the proposed remedy

- [ ] Check for handling a phone complaint without preserving a written claim
- [ ] Check for combining several items into one unverifiable amount
- [ ] Check for losing pickup-condition evidence
- [ ] Check for treating an acknowledgment as a final decision

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Complete-claim time, Decision cycle time, Reopened decision rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
