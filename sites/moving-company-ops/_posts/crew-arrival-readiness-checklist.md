A checklist for moving crew arrival readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent household moving companies and local moving crews and centers on one result: **every dispatched crew leaves with a confirmed job scope, access plan, equipment load, and customer arrival promise**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Move, date, and service type
- Confirm Origin and destination contacts
- Confirm Address, parking, stairs, and access windows
- Confirm Current inventory and special items

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Review the next move against the schedule
- Update Confirm customer, address, and access details
- Update Match crew, vehicle, and equipment to scope
- Update Resolve missing documents or readiness exceptions
- Update Release dispatch and communicate arrival

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Crew roles and qualifications
- Verify Vehicle and equipment load
- Verify Required job documents
- Verify Customer confirmation and dispatch release

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a scheduled move nears the readiness cutoff
- [ ] Review records where customer or building access details change
- [ ] Review records where assigned crew, vehicle, or required equipment becomes unavailable

- [ ] Check for dispatching from an outdated estimate
- [ ] Check for assuming building access from a prior move
- [ ] Check for loading equipment without matching the special-item list
- [ ] Check for promising arrival before crew and vehicle are released

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time dispatch readiness, Arrival delay causes, Day-of scope surprise rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
