Examples make community theater rehearsal conflict tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases community theaters and volunteer-led stage-production teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A lead becomes unavailable for a blocking rehearsal

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Production rehearsal and schedule version, Conflict source role and timing, Scenes numbers and required participants, then move it through register the conflict against the current schedule and identify scenes roles staff and rooms affected. If a cast crew room or director conflict is reported, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: The stage is lost to another event

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Conflict source role and timing, Scenes numbers and required participants, Room staff and technical dependencies, then move it through register the conflict against the current schedule and identify scenes roles staff and rooms affected. If a resolution changes another role or scene call, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A fight call requires participants not on the original schedule

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Scenes numbers and required participants, Room staff and technical dependencies, Resolution options and director decision, then move it through register the conflict against the current schedule and identify scenes roles staff and rooms affected. If an affected participant misses the acknowledgment cutoff, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open rehearsal conflict needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every material rehearsal conflict is resolved against scene and role dependencies, published as one current schedule, and acknowledged by affected participants?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the theater audition, cast, rehearsal, scene, volunteer, inventory, and production platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
