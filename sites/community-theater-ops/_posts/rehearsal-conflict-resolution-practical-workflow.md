Cast and crew availability, scene requirements, music or fight calls, room access, volunteer schedules, and newly added conflicts cause calendar changes that do not reach everyone affected. For community theaters and volunteer-led stage-production teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **every material rehearsal conflict is resolved against scene and role dependencies, published as one current schedule, and acknowledged by affected participants**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Every open rehearsal conflict needs one owner and a next review time
- Completion requires recorded evidence that every material rehearsal conflict is resolved against scene and role dependencies, published as one current schedule, and acknowledged by affected participants
- Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason
- Keep the theater audition, cast, rehearsal, scene, volunteer, inventory, and production platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Register the conflict against the current schedule

Record **Production rehearsal and schedule version** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can identify scenes roles staff and rooms affected, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Identify scenes roles staff and rooms affected

Record **Conflict source role and timing** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can compare approved resolution options, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Compare approved resolution options

Record **Scenes numbers and required participants** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can publish the revised call and supersede old versions, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Publish the revised call and supersede old versions

Record **Room staff and technical dependencies** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can collect acknowledgments and handle remaining exceptions, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Collect acknowledgments and handle remaining exceptions

Record **Resolution options and director decision** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a cast crew room or director conflict is reported
- a resolution changes another role or scene call
- an affected participant misses the acknowledgment cutoff

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
