A checklist for auto repair parts arrival and customer promise tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent auto repair shops and service-advisor teams and centers on one result: **every ordered part has a verified ETA, affected repair order, customer promise, and exception owner**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Repair order and vehicle
- Confirm Part number and description
- Confirm Supplier and purchase order
- Confirm Quantity ordered and received

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Link the ordered part to the repair order
- Update Record supplier confirmation and ETA
- Update Check arrival against the customer promise
- Update Handle delay, substitution, or partial delivery
- Update Confirm receipt and release the next shop action

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Confirmed ETA
- Verify Customer promise date
- Verify Exception owner and next check
- Verify Receipt or substitution evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a supplier changes or misses the confirmed eta
- [ ] Review records where only part of an order arrives
- [ ] Review records where a substitute changes cost, fitment, or warranty

- [ ] Check for repeating an unconfirmed supplier eta to the customer
- [ ] Check for marking a multi-part order complete after a partial delivery
- [ ] Check for failing to connect a substitute part to the revised authorization
- [ ] Check for leaving the customer promise unchanged after a delay

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are ETA reliability, Parts-blocked repair age, Promise revision rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
