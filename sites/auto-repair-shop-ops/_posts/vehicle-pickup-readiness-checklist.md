A checklist for auto repair vehicle pickup readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent auto repair shops and service-advisor teams and centers on one result: **every completed vehicle is released only after the handoff checks and customer pickup plan are confirmed**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Repair order and vehicle
- Confirm Final quality-check result
- Confirm Open warning or comeback note
- Confirm Invoice and payment status

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Flag mechanical work as complete
- Update Run the final quality and documentation check
- Update Prepare invoice, keys, and vehicle location
- Update Confirm the pickup plan with the customer
- Update Record vehicle release and remaining commitments

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Keys and parking location
- Verify Customer notification evidence
- Verify Pickup window and method
- Verify Release time and recipient

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where mechanical work completes but a readiness check is still open
- [ ] Review records where the customer changes the pickup person or time
- [ ] Review records where payment, keys, or final documentation is missing at arrival

- [ ] Check for texting the customer before the final quality check passes
- [ ] Check for using paid as a substitute for recording who received the vehicle
- [ ] Check for forgetting after-hours key instructions
- [ ] Check for hiding an unresolved warning in a technician note

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-to-notified time, Ready vehicle dwell, Pickup exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
