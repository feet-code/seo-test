A checklist for freight carrier packet completeness tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small freight brokerages and shipper-carrier coordination teams and centers on one result: **every carrier assigned to a load has current required evidence, approved exceptions, and a verified qualification decision**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Carrier legal name and identifier
- Confirm Authority status and checked time
- Confirm Insurance type, limit, and expiry
- Confirm Agreement and tax-form status

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create requirements from carrier and load context
- Update Collect submitted business documents
- Update Verify authoritative status and document dates
- Update Route exceptions to authorized review
- Update Record qualification and release or block assignment

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Payment-profile status
- Verify Load-specific requirement
- Verify Reviewer and exception approval
- Verify Qualified-until date and decision evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a new carrier is considered for a load
- [ ] Review records where required authority, insurance, agreement, or verification expires or changes
- [ ] Review records where a load needs a client-specific qualification exception

- [ ] Check for trusting an uploaded certificate without verification
- [ ] Check for keeping sensitive payment details in a broad spreadsheet
- [ ] Check for treating prior use as current qualification
- [ ] Check for allowing a one-load exception to become permanent

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-on-first-review, Qualification lead time, Expiring assignment exposure. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
