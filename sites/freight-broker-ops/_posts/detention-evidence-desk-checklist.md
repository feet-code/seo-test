A checklist for freight detention evidence tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small freight brokerages and shipper-carrier coordination teams and centers on one result: **every detention request is evaluated from agreed terms and time evidence, then reconciled across customer charge and carrier payment**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Load, stop, facility, and parties
- Confirm Appointment and appointment type
- Confirm Arrival, check-in, dock, and release times
- Confirm Free-time and rate terms

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the request against the load and stop
- Update Reconstruct appointment, arrival, release, and free time
- Update Collect facility and driver evidence
- Update Approve, revise, or deny the accessorial
- Update Reconcile customer billing, carrier payment, and communication

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Tracking, BOL, or facility evidence
- Verify Delay cause and exception
- Verify Customer decision and amount
- Verify Carrier payment and billing reconciliation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a driver reports delay beyond free time
- [ ] Review records where tracking and paperwork show different arrival or release times
- [ ] Review records where customer decision or new evidence changes the approved amount

- [ ] Check for using a driver text as the only time source
- [ ] Check for applying the wrong customer's free-time terms
- [ ] Check for approving carrier payment without customer-billing disposition
- [ ] Check for changing timestamps after a decision without history

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Evidence-complete time, Decision cycle time, Recovery reconciliation. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
