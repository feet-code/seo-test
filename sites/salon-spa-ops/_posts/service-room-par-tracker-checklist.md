A checklist for salon and spa room inventory par tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent salons, spas, and small wellness studios and centers on one result: **each service room is replenished to an agreed par before its next booked service without hiding inventory variance**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Location and service room
- Confirm Supply item and unit
- Confirm Par and reorder threshold
- Confirm Counted quantity and time

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Set par levels by room and service
- Update Record the room count at the operating cadence
- Update Create replenishment work for shortages
- Update Resolve stockout, transfer, or count variance
- Update Confirm the room is ready and update central stock

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Upcoming service demand
- Verify Replenishment quantity
- Verify Owner and source location
- Verify Completion or variance evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a count falls below par before a booked service
- [ ] Review records where central stock cannot fulfill the replenishment quantity
- [ ] Review records where verified usage differs materially from expected usage

- [ ] Check for using purchase units and service units interchangeably
- [ ] Check for refilling a room without reducing central stock
- [ ] Check for raising par to hide unexplained usage
- [ ] Check for closing a task before the item reaches the room

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Room readiness rate, Stockout incidents, Inventory variance. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
