Examples make salon and spa room inventory par tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent salons, spas, and small wellness studios can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A facial room has one mask left before a fully booked afternoon

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Location and service room, Supply item and unit, Par and reorder threshold, then move it through set par levels by room and service and record the room count at the operating cadence. If a count falls below par before a booked service, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Wax is moved between rooms but central stock is not updated

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Supply item and unit, Par and reorder threshold, Counted quantity and time, then move it through set par levels by room and service and record the room count at the operating cadence. If central stock cannot fulfill the replenishment quantity, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: Glove usage rises after a new service protocol

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Par and reorder threshold, Counted quantity and time, Upcoming service demand, then move it through set par levels by room and service and record the room count at the operating cadence. If verified usage differs materially from expected usage, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open service-room replenishment task needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that each service room is replenished to an agreed par before its next booked service without hiding inventory variance?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep booking and point-of-sale platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
