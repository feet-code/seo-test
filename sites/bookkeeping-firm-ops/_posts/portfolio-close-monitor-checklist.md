A checklist for bookkeeping month-end close checklist and portfolio tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small bookkeeping firms and client accounting service teams and centers on one result: **the firm can identify the next action and delivery risk for every client close without reconstructing status manually**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client and period
- Confirm Task template
- Confirm Preparer
- Confirm Reviewer
- Confirm Due date

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Instantiate the client close checklist
- Update Complete preparer tasks with evidence
- Update Resolve exceptions and missing inputs
- Update Complete reviewer sign-off
- Update Deliver and close the period

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Evidence link
- Verify Exception
- Verify Waiting reason
- Verify Review status
- Verify Delivery date

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a critical task passes its internal due date
- [ ] Review records where a close waits on client input or ledger correction
- [ ] Review records where review workload exceeds the remaining delivery window

- [ ] Check for marking tasks complete without evidence
- [ ] Check for using percent complete when one critical blocker remains
- [ ] Check for sending every task to review at the same deadline
- [ ] Check for changing a recurring template without recording why

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time close rate, Review queue age, Exception recurrence. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
