Firm owners cannot see which client closes are truly at risk without opening multiple checklists, inboxes, and accounting files. For small bookkeeping firms and client accounting service teams, the useful goal is not to add another dashboard. It is to create a small, visible process that produces this outcome: **the firm can identify the next action and delivery risk for every client close without reconstructing status manually**.

## Define the finish line first

Write the outcome in operational terms. A teammate should be able to look at one record and tell what happened, who owns the next action, and what is blocking completion. Use these rules as the boundary:

- Task completion includes evidence
- Critical path matters more than percent complete
- Preparer and reviewer ownership are separate
- Recurring exceptions feed back into next period's template

## A practical end-to-end workflow

### 1. Instantiate the client close checklist

Record **Client and period** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can complete preparer tasks with evidence, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 2. Complete preparer tasks with evidence

Record **Task template** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can resolve exceptions and missing inputs, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 3. Resolve exceptions and missing inputs

Record **Preparer** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can complete reviewer sign-off, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 4. Complete reviewer sign-off

Record **Reviewer** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can deliver and close the period, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

### 5. Deliver and close the period

Record **Due date** at this point, name the person responsible, and define the evidence that allows the work to move to the next step. The exit condition should be observable: either the team can close the record and review the outcome, or the record remains open with a reason and next action.

## Handle exceptions without hiding them

The process needs an explicit waiting state. Do not mark work complete because a reminder was sent or a form was opened. Keep the record open until the real exit condition is met. These events deserve a named exception path:

- a critical task passes its internal due date
- a close waits on client input or ledger correction
- review workload exceeds the remaining delivery window

For each exception, store the reason, the next review date, and the person who can unblock it. That makes a weekly review useful instead of turning it into a search across email, chat, and spreadsheets.

## Start with one live cycle

Run the workflow for one client, location, role, order, or participant before standardizing it. At the end, remove fields nobody used, add evidence that was missing, and keep the status list short. The workflow is ready to scale when another person can operate it without asking the original owner what each row means.
