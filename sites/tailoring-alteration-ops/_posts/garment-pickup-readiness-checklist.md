A checklist for alteration garment pickup readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent tailoring, alteration, and garment-repair shops and centers on one result: **every finished garment is checked against approved work, packaged with customer property, financially reconciled, and staged before notification**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer order and garment identifiers
- Confirm Approved alteration lines and version
- Confirm Final workmanship and measurement checks
- Confirm Pressing cleaning and packaging

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Compare completed work with the current ticket
- Update Inspect fit workmanship finish and pressing
- Update Gather accessories remnants and related garments
- Update Reconcile invoice deposit and collector authority
- Update Package stage notify and record release

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Accessories buttons belts and remnants
- Verify Invoice deposit discount and balance
- Verify Authorized collector and notification
- Verify Rack location release time and exception

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where production marks the garment complete
- [ ] Review records where quality review finds a defect or missing item
- [ ] Review records where the customer changes collector or pickup time

- [ ] Check for sending notification when sewing ends
- [ ] Check for checking against the original rather than latest ticket
- [ ] Check for storing a belt or spare fabric separately
- [ ] Check for releasing without recording who collected the garment

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-on-first-notice rate, Finish-to-stage time, Pickup exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
