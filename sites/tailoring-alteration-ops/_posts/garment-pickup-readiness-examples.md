Examples make alteration garment pickup readiness easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent tailoring, alteration, and garment-repair shops can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A dress is finished but the matching sash is elsewhere

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer order and garment identifiers, Approved alteration lines and version, Final workmanship and measurement checks, then move it through compare completed work with the current ticket and inspect fit workmanship finish and pressing. If production marks the garment complete, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Pressing reveals a seam pucker

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Approved alteration lines and version, Final workmanship and measurement checks, Pressing cleaning and packaging, then move it through compare completed work with the current ticket and inspect fit workmanship finish and pressing. If quality review finds a defect or missing item, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A family member collects a suit for the customer

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Final workmanship and measurement checks, Pressing cleaning and packaging, Accessories buttons belts and remnants, then move it through compare completed work with the current ticket and inspect fit workmanship finish and pressing. If the customer changes collector or pickup time, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open garment release needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every finished garment is checked against approved work, packaged with customer property, financially reconciled, and staged before notification?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the tailor-shop pos, customer, measurement, garment, fitting, order, and payment platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
