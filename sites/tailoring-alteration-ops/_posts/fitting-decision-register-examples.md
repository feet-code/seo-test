Examples make tailoring fitting change approval tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent tailoring, alteration, and garment-repair shops can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A jacket sleeve change alters button placement

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer garment and order, Fitting number date and fitter, Garment measurements and marked locations, then move it through prepare the current garment and prior plan and capture fit observations and requested changes. If a fitting changes the approved alteration plan, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A dress hem changes after shoes are selected

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Fitting number date and fitter, Garment measurements and marked locations, Customer fit observations, then move it through prepare the current garment and prior plan and capture fit observations and requested changes. If price or promised date is affected, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A rush request removes time for a planned second fitting

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Garment measurements and marked locations, Customer fit observations, Approved alteration lines and tolerances, then move it through prepare the current garment and prior plan and capture fit observations and requested changes. If the sewer finds instructions inconsistent with garment markings, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open fitting decision needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every fitting produces an agreed current alteration plan, price or date consequence, garment marking reference, and next checkpoint?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the tailor-shop pos, customer, measurement, garment, fitting, order, and payment platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
