A checklist for tailoring fitting change approval tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent tailoring, alteration, and garment-repair shops and centers on one result: **every fitting produces an agreed current alteration plan, price or date consequence, garment marking reference, and next checkpoint**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer garment and order
- Confirm Fitting number date and fitter
- Confirm Garment measurements and marked locations
- Confirm Customer fit observations

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Prepare the current garment and prior plan
- Update Capture fit observations and requested changes
- Update Translate decisions into specific alteration work
- Update Confirm price date and customer approval
- Update Publish the new version for sewing or next fitting

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Approved alteration lines and tolerances
- Verify Price and due-date change
- Verify Customer approval evidence
- Verify Pattern ticket version and next appointment

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a fitting changes the approved alteration plan
- [ ] Review records where price or promised date is affected
- [ ] Review records where the sewer finds instructions inconsistent with garment markings

- [ ] Check for writing fits better with no marked location
- [ ] Check for erasing a prior decision after a later fitting
- [ ] Check for adding work without price or deadline discussion
- [ ] Check for starting final finishing before approval is clear

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Fitting-to-release time, Unplanned refit rate, Change dispute rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
