A checklist for conference speaker asset tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent conference organizers and small trade-show teams and centers on one result: **every confirmed speaker has the approved assets and permissions required for agenda publication, production, and session delivery**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Event, session, and speaker
- Confirm Session title and abstract
- Confirm Biography and headshot
- Confirm Release or recording permission

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create the asset packet from the session commitment
- Update Request only the required speaker items
- Update Validate format, content, and permissions
- Update Resolve revisions and freeze approved versions
- Update Publish to agenda and hand off production assets

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Slide or file requirement
- Verify Travel and AV requirement status
- Verify Owner, due date, and review decision
- Verify Approved version and publication destination

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a speaker is confirmed or session requirements change
- [ ] Review records where an asset fails format, permission, or content review
- [ ] Review records where publication or production cutoff approaches with open items

- [ ] Check for publishing a submitted bio before approval
- [ ] Check for using a headshot with unclear rights
- [ ] Check for chasing slides for a session that does not require them
- [ ] Check for letting agenda and production use different title versions

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-by-publication rate, Asset first-pass acceptance, Production hold time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
