Examples make conference speaker asset tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent conference organizers and small trade-show teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A headshot is too small for signage

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Event, session, and speaker, Session title and abstract, Biography and headshot, then move it through create the asset packet from the session commitment and request only the required speaker items. If a speaker is confirmed or session requirements change, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A session title differs between slides and agenda

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Session title and abstract, Biography and headshot, Release or recording permission, then move it through create the asset packet from the session commitment and request only the required speaker items. If an asset fails format, permission, or content review, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: Recording permission remains undecided before livestream setup

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Biography and headshot, Release or recording permission, Slide or file requirement, then move it through create the asset packet from the session commitment and request only the required speaker items. If publication or production cutoff approaches with open items, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open speaker asset requirement needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every confirmed speaker has the approved assets and permissions required for agenda publication, production, and session delivery?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the event agenda, speaker, sponsor, registration, and contract platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
