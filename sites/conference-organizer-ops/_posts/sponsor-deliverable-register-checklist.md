A checklist for conference sponsor deliverable tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent conference organizers and small trade-show teams and centers on one result: **every contracted sponsor obligation has an approved input, delivery owner, placement evidence, and accepted outcome**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Event, sponsor, and package
- Confirm Contract obligation
- Confirm Sponsor input and due date
- Confirm Internal owner and dependency

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Extract obligations and inputs from the signed package
- Update Assign sponsor contact and internal delivery owner
- Update Collect and approve required assets
- Update Execute and evidence each placement or entitlement
- Update Reconcile delivery and issue the sponsor report

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Asset approval status
- Verify Delivery date and placement
- Verify Evidence link
- Verify Sponsor acceptance, exception, or make-good

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a sponsor input approaches its production cutoff
- [ ] Review records where a placement or entitlement becomes infeasible
- [ ] Review records where delivery is marked complete without evidence or sponsor acceptance

- [ ] Check for tracking invoice payment but not fulfillment
- [ ] Check for treating a logo upload as placement evidence
- [ ] Check for changing an entitlement without approved make-good
- [ ] Check for building the post-event report from memory

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Obligation completion, Sponsor input timeliness, Evidence completeness. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
