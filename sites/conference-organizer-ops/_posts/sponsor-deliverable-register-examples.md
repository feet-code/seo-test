Examples make conference sponsor deliverable tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent conference organizers and small trade-show teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A sponsor logo misses the print deadline

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Event, sponsor, and package, Contract obligation, Sponsor input and due date, then move it through extract obligations and inputs from the signed package and assign sponsor contact and internal delivery owner. If a sponsor input approaches its production cutoff, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A contracted session slot changes rooms

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Contract obligation, Sponsor input and due date, Internal owner and dependency, then move it through extract obligations and inputs from the signed package and assign sponsor contact and internal delivery owner. If a placement or entitlement becomes infeasible, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: Lead-scan reporting needs evidence from the event platform

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Sponsor input and due date, Internal owner and dependency, Asset approval status, then move it through extract obligations and inputs from the signed package and assign sponsor contact and internal delivery owner. If delivery is marked complete without evidence or sponsor acceptance, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open sponsor obligation needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every contracted sponsor obligation has an approved input, delivery owner, placement evidence, and accepted outcome?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the event agenda, speaker, sponsor, registration, and contract platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
