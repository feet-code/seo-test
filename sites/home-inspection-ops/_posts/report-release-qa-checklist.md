A checklist for home inspection report quality review should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent home inspection companies and small multi-inspector teams and centers on one result: **every inspection report is released only after an accountable inspector reviews identity, completeness, consistency, media, recommendations, and client delivery**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client property inspection and inspector
- Confirm Template and report version
- Confirm Required systems areas and limitations
- Confirm Observations locations and recommendations

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Complete field capture and draft observations
- Update Run structural completeness and consistency checks
- Update Review every flagged item and automated suggestion
- Update Approve the final report as the responsible inspector
- Update Deliver verify access and preserve the released version

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Photos videos annotations and links
- Verify Placeholders contradictions and flags
- Verify Inspector approval time and signature
- Verify Delivery recipients access evidence and amendment history

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where field capture is marked complete
- [ ] Review records where automated checks find missing or conflicting content
- [ ] Review records where a delivered report requires a correction or clarification

- [ ] Check for auto-publishing generated observations
- [ ] Check for removing a limitation because no defect was found
- [ ] Check for fixing contradictory language in only the summary
- [ ] Check for editing the report after delivery without an amendment record

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Inspection-to-release time, First-release correction rate, Flag resolution rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
