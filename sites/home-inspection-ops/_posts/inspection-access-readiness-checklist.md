A checklist for home inspection property access readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent home inspection companies and small multi-inspector teams and centers on one result: **every inspection starts with property-specific access, utilities, scope, agreement, payment, and contacts confirmed or a documented limitation plan**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client property and services
- Confirm Date inspector and expected duration
- Confirm Entry method agent and onsite contacts
- Confirm Electric water gas and system status

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create readiness requirements from property and service
- Update Confirm access utilities and occupied constraints
- Update Collect agreement payment and contacts
- Update Review unresolved limitations before travel
- Update Release the appointment and current inspector packet

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Attic crawlspace outbuilding and occupied access
- Verify Agreement scope and payment status
- Verify Known limitation response and client notice
- Verify Reviewer release and packet version

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an inspection is scheduled or rescheduled
- [ ] Review records where agent seller or client reports an access change
- [ ] Review records where a blocking readiness item remains open at travel cutoff

- [ ] Check for assuming lockbox access includes every area
- [ ] Check for treating utilities on as a generic checkbox
- [ ] Check for letting the inspector discover an unsigned agreement onsite
- [ ] Check for promising inspection of an inaccessible component

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-before-travel rate, Access limitation rate, Avoided trip rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
