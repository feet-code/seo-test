A checklist for self-storage delinquency follow-up tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent self-storage facilities and small multi-site operators and centers on one result: **every delinquent account has a policy-based next action, documented tenant response, and verified stop condition**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Facility, tenant, unit, and lease
- Confirm Balance and aging date
- Confirm Policy version and current milestone
- Confirm Notice channel and delivery evidence

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the delinquency action from the account ledger
- Update Apply the current facility policy and milestone
- Update Contact the tenant through the approved channel
- Update Record a payment, promise, dispute, move-out, or escalation
- Update Verify the ledger and access outcome before closure

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Tenant response and promise date
- Verify Manager exception and approval
- Verify Access or move-out status
- Verify Payment evidence or next review

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a balance reaches the next policy milestone
- [ ] Review records where a tenant makes or misses a payment promise
- [ ] Review records where payment, access, or move-out status changes in another system

- [ ] Check for keeping a tenant promise only in call notes
- [ ] Check for changing access before the required policy milestone
- [ ] Check for continuing reminders after payment posts
- [ ] Check for granting an exception without recording who approved it

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Promise-kept rate, Open delinquency age, Ledger-to-action accuracy. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
