Examples make self-storage delinquency follow-up tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent self-storage facilities and small multi-site operators can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A tenant promises Friday payment after a reminder

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Facility, tenant, unit, and lease, Balance and aging date, Policy version and current milestone, then move it through open the delinquency action from the account ledger and apply the current facility policy and milestone. If a balance reaches the next policy milestone, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: An online payment posts after an access action was queued

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Balance and aging date, Policy version and current milestone, Notice channel and delivery evidence, then move it through open the delinquency action from the account ledger and apply the current facility policy and milestone. If a tenant makes or misses a payment promise, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A manager approves a move-out resolution instead of another collection step

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Policy version and current milestone, Notice channel and delivery evidence, Tenant response and promise date, then move it through open the delinquency action from the account ledger and apply the current facility policy and milestone. If payment, access, or move-out status changes in another system, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open delinquent tenant action needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every delinquent account has a policy-based next action, documented tenant response, and verified stop condition?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the facility-management, access, lease, and payment platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
