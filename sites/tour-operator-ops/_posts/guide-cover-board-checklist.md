A checklist for tour guide scheduling and substitution should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small day-tour, activity, and multi-day tour operators and centers on one result: **every uncovered departure is accepted by a qualified guide or escalated to a documented operating decision before the guest notice cutoff**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Tour, departure, and meeting point
- Confirm Original guide and exception
- Confirm Required qualification and language
- Confirm Available candidate guides

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the coverage exception against the departure
- Update Identify qualified and available guides
- Update Offer and confirm the assignment
- Update Transfer manifest, access, and resource instructions
- Update Verify guide acceptance and publish the operating plan

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Confirmed guide and acceptance time
- Verify Pay or schedule adjustment
- Verify Manifest and resource handoff
- Verify Guest notice or cancellation evidence

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an assigned guide becomes unavailable
- [ ] Review records where no qualified guide accepts by the escalation time
- [ ] Review records where the replacement cannot access the current manifest or resources

- [ ] Check for assigning the first respondent without checking qualification
- [ ] Check for updating the public schedule before acceptance
- [ ] Check for forgetting transport or equipment access
- [ ] Check for assuming sent manifest means the substitute reviewed it

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Coverage fill time, Qualified coverage rate, Late operating-change rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
