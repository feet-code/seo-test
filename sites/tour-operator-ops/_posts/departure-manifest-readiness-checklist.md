A checklist for tour departure manifest readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for small day-tour, activity, and multi-day tour operators and centers on one result: **every departure has one frozen operational manifest with resolved blocking fields and controlled late changes**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Tour, departure, and capacity
- Confirm Participant and booking status
- Confirm Pickup or meeting point
- Confirm Required waiver or form status

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Create the departure roster from confirmed bookings
- Update Validate participant and operational requirements
- Update Assign pickup, equipment, and resource details
- Update Resolve missing data and capacity exceptions
- Update Freeze, distribute, and control late manifest changes

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Equipment or size requirement
- Verify Operational note and access scope
- Verify Guide and vehicle assignment
- Verify Manifest version, freeze time, and late change

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a departure approaches its freeze time
- [ ] Review records where capacity, participant status, pickup, or resource assignment changes
- [ ] Review records where a blocking waiver, field, or payment state remains open

- [ ] Check for exporting a manifest before payment and cancellation states settle
- [ ] Check for sharing private participant notes beyond the guide's need
- [ ] Check for editing a printed manifest with no version control
- [ ] Check for treating waitlisted guests as confirmed capacity

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Ready-at-freeze rate, Late manifest changes, Check-in exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
