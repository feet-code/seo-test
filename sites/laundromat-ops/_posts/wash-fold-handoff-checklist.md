A checklist for laundromat wash dry fold order tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent laundromats offering self-service and wash-dry-fold and centers on one result: **every wash-dry-fold order preserves customer instructions and bag identity through processing, assembly, quality check, payment, and authorized release**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer order and contact
- Confirm Intake weight bags and labels
- Confirm Preferences exclusions and promised time
- Confirm Machine assignments and operators

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Accept weigh label and document the order
- Update Assign loads while preserving order identity
- Update Record wash dry and exception decisions
- Update Assemble weigh and quality-check every piece or bag
- Update Notify collect payment and record release

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Stage times products and exceptions
- Verify Final weight bags and quality check
- Verify Price payment and notification
- Verify Collector authority release time and discrepancy

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a drop-off order is accepted
- [ ] Review records where a load is split delayed or produces an exception
- [ ] Review records where a customer or collector arrives before release readiness

- [ ] Check for combining customer loads without an identity control
- [ ] Check for recording preferences only on a paper ticket
- [ ] Check for marking complete before all split loads are assembled
- [ ] Check for releasing to someone without order or customer verification

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time ready rate, Weight variance, Rework or claim rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
