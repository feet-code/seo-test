A checklist for laundromat washer and dryer outage tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent laundromats offering self-service and wash-dry-fold and centers on one result: **every machine outage has immediate customer containment, repair ownership, status visibility, and a documented loaded-cycle return test**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Store machine and payment identifier
- Confirm Fault time symptoms and reporter
- Confirm Affected cycle customer and payment
- Confirm Containment sign and remote-disable state

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Record machine fault and customer impact
- Update Disable use and handle affected payment
- Update Diagnose or dispatch the repair
- Update Update attendants and expected availability
- Update Run the required test and restore service

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Diagnostic code photos and history
- Verify Owner vendor part and ETA
- Verify Attendant update and next review
- Verify Test cycle evidence and restored time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a customer attendant or telemetry reports a fault
- [ ] Review records where repair diagnosis eta or payment impact changes
- [ ] Review records where the machine fails its return test

- [ ] Check for posting a sign without blocking app selection
- [ ] Check for refunding a customer without linking the machine fault
- [ ] Check for marking fixed when a vendor leaves
- [ ] Check for testing empty when the failure appears only under load

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Containment time, Verified downtime, Repeat-outage rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
