Unavailable stems, color variation, quality rejection, seasonality, and wholesaler shortages force substitutions, but design intent, price, client approval, and recipe updates may not stay aligned. The recurring failures are usually process-design problems rather than motivation problems. For independent retail, delivery, and event floral studios, these are the mistakes worth finding before buying or building software.


### 1. Substituting by color alone without form or mechanics

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Original stem color grade and quantity** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Completion requires recorded evidence that every material substitution preserves design intent and margin with documented internal or client approval before production When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 2. Asking the client to decide without curated options

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Shortage source and quality evidence** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 3. Updating purchasing but not the recipe

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Substitute options and visual reference** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Keep the florist POS, proposal, recipe, stem inventory, order, route, and event platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

### 4. Absorbing material cost without recording margin impact

This usually survives because the workflow records activity but not the decision that activity was meant to produce. Add **Recipe mechanics and palette impact** at the point of work and enforce this guardrail: Every open floral substitution needs one owner and a next review time When the exception occurs, keep it visible instead of repairing it privately in email.

## Audit five recent records

Pick five completed or abandoned examples and ask:

- Can we reconstruct client event order and arrangement without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct original stem color grade and quantity without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct shortage source and quality evidence without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct substitute options and visual reference without asking the original owner?
- Can we reconstruct recipe mechanics and palette impact without asking the original owner?

If the answer is no, improve the capture point rather than adding a later reporting step. Reports cannot recover decisions that were never recorded.

## Use mistakes as software requirements

Turn every frequent failure into a testable requirement. “Better visibility” is vague; “show every record with no owner or next date” can be tested. “More automation” is vague; “stop reminders after the completion condition is recorded” can be tested.
