A checklist for florist delivery and event installation readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent retail, delivery, and event floral studios and centers on one result: **every delivery or installation departs with complete counted product, compatible transport, confirmed access, assigned crew, and proof requirements**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Client event venue and order version
- Confirm Arrangement and rental item counts
- Confirm Condition photos labels and temperature needs
- Confirm Vehicle load order and route

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Freeze the current order and production list
- Update Count inspect label and stage arrangements
- Update Confirm vehicle route venue and contact
- Update Brief crew on setup strike and proof
- Update Release departure and reconcile completion

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Venue access dock stairs and window
- Verify Onsite contact crew and setup plan
- Verify Proof delivery and strike requirements
- Verify Departure arrival completion and exceptions

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where production reaches staging cutoff
- [ ] Review records where venue access timing or order changes
- [ ] Review records where crew completion reveals a missing or damaged item

- [ ] Check for counting arrangements without rental mechanics
- [ ] Check for loading delicate work before vehicle conditions are ready
- [ ] Check for assuming venue access from a prior event
- [ ] Check for marking delivered before setup and proof are complete

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Complete-departure rate, Arrival-to-ready time, Delivery exception rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
