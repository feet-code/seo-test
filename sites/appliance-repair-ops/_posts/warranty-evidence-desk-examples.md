Examples make appliance repair warranty claim evidence tracking easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent appliance repair companies and small authorized-service teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A claim lacks the appliance serial photo

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Manufacturer claim and dispatch, Customer appliance model and serial, Coverage and authorization number, then move it through register warranty dispatch and requirements and capture diagnosis service and authorization evidence. If a warranty dispatch or authorization arrives, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: Authorized labor differs from actual time

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer appliance model and serial, Coverage and authorization number, Complaint diagnosis codes and photos, then move it through register warranty dispatch and requirements and capture diagnosis service and authorization evidence. If completed work is missing a claim requirement, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: A replaced part requires return tracking before payment

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Coverage and authorization number, Complaint diagnosis codes and photos, Parts numbers disposition and receipts, then move it through register warranty dispatch and requirements and capture diagnosis service and authorization evidence. If the manufacturer requests correction or denies payment, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open warranty claim needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every warranty job reaches submission with complete authorized evidence and remains visible until reimbursement, correction, or documented denial?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the appliance-service crm, dispatch, model, diagnosis, parts, warranty, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
