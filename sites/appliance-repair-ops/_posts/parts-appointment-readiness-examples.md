Examples make appliance repair parts appointment readiness easier to design because they reveal where a neat diagram meets messy work. The scenarios below are not claims about a particular company; they are test cases independent appliance repair companies and small authorized-service teams can run against a template or software trial.

### Scenario 1: A control board arrives for the wrong revision

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Customer appliance and service job, Brand model serial and diagnosis, Part number revision and source, then move it through review diagnosis authorization and required parts and verify received identity compatibility and condition. If a required part is ordered or received, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 2: A stacked dryer requires a second technician

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Brand model serial and diagnosis, Part number revision and source, Order received and inspected state, then move it through review diagnosis authorization and required parts and verify received identity compatibility and condition. If part job technician or customer status changes, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason. ### Scenario 3: The tenant has changed since diagnosis and access needs reconfirmation

Create the record before the first follow-up. Capture Part number revision and source, Order received and inspected state, Authorization warranty and remaining balance, then move it through review diagnosis authorization and required parts and verify received identity compatibility and condition. If the appointment nears cutoff without all readiness evidence, do not improvise in a private message; assign the exception, set a review date, and preserve the evidence needed for the next decision. Close with an explicit outcome and reason.

## Debrief each scenario

After running a scenario, ask:

- Did the record make every open return repair appointment needs one owner and a next review time?
- Did the record make completion requires recorded evidence that every parts-dependent appointment is released only after the exact usable parts, job scope, technician capability, and customer access are confirmed?
- Did the record make automated reminders stop after verified completion or a documented closed reason?
- Did the record make keep the appliance-service crm, dispatch, model, diagnosis, parts, warranty, and billing platform as the system of record; only necessary coordination data belongs here?

Also check whether a new teammate could identify the owner, next action, and finish condition without opening another system.

## Convert scenarios into acceptance tests

Use the normal case, waiting case, and closed-without-completion case in every software demo. Require the vendor—or your own prototype—to show the full workflow rather than isolated feature screens. Export the resulting records and verify that the status history remains understandable.
