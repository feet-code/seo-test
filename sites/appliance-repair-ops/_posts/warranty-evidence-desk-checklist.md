A checklist for appliance repair warranty claim evidence tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent appliance repair companies and small authorized-service teams and centers on one result: **every warranty job reaches submission with complete authorized evidence and remains visible until reimbursement, correction, or documented denial**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Manufacturer claim and dispatch
- Confirm Customer appliance model and serial
- Confirm Coverage and authorization number
- Confirm Complaint diagnosis codes and photos

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Register warranty dispatch and requirements
- Update Capture diagnosis service and authorization evidence
- Update Validate claim fields against completed work
- Update Submit and track acknowledgment or correction
- Update Reconcile payment denial or appeal and close

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Parts numbers disposition and receipts
- Verify Labor travel and allowance
- Verify Customer signature invoice and submission
- Verify Response correction reimbursement and close reason

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a warranty dispatch or authorization arrives
- [ ] Review records where completed work is missing a claim requirement
- [ ] Review records where the manufacturer requests correction or denies payment

- [ ] Check for starting covered work before required authorization
- [ ] Check for using a technician note where a diagnostic code is required
- [ ] Check for closing the job when the customer repair ends
- [ ] Check for appealing a denial without preserving the submitted version

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are First-pass acceptance, Submission lag, Reimbursement cycle. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
