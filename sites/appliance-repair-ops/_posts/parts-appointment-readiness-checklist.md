A checklist for appliance repair parts appointment readiness should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent appliance repair companies and small authorized-service teams and centers on one result: **every parts-dependent appointment is released only after the exact usable parts, job scope, technician capability, and customer access are confirmed**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Customer appliance and service job
- Confirm Brand model serial and diagnosis
- Confirm Part number revision and source
- Confirm Order received and inspected state

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Review diagnosis authorization and required parts
- Update Verify received identity compatibility and condition
- Update Match technician tools and estimated work
- Update Confirm customer access and appliance state
- Update Release the appointment with the current job packet

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Authorization warranty and remaining balance
- Verify Technician skill tools and duration
- Verify Customer access utilities and appointment
- Verify Reviewer release and packet version

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a required part is ordered or received
- [ ] Review records where part job technician or customer status changes
- [ ] Review records where the appointment nears cutoff without all readiness evidence

- [ ] Check for scheduling from a tracking eta
- [ ] Check for checking the box without matching model revision
- [ ] Check for sending a technician without specialized tool requirement
- [ ] Check for ignoring that the appliance or access condition changed

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are First-return completion rate, Received-to-scheduled time, Wrong-part rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
