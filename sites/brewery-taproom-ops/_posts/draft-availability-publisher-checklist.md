A checklist for brewery tap list availability tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent craft breweries operating one or more taprooms and centers on one result: **every draft availability change is approved, published across intended channels, verified live, and reactivated only from confirmed product and line readiness**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Taproom line beer and batch
- Confirm Change reason time and reporter
- Confirm Keg quantity inventory and hold state
- Confirm Expected return and replacement option

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Open the beer and line availability change
- Update Confirm inventory hold and expected duration
- Update Approve replacement wording and sales behavior
- Update Publish across POS boards web and staff
- Update Verify live state and schedule reactivation review

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Affected POS board web and menu channels
- Verify Approver publisher and staff notice
- Verify Live verification evidence
- Verify Reactivation owner condition and time

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where a keg kicks or beer is held
- [ ] Review records where one guest-facing channel differs from approved state
- [ ] Review records where verified keg and line readiness supports reactivation

- [ ] Check for removing a menu item but leaving pos sale enabled
- [ ] Check for replacing beer without checking line or allergen notes
- [ ] Check for reactivating from expected keg arrival
- [ ] Check for letting each shift maintain a separate tap list

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are Channel consistency time, Incorrect-sale attempts, Reactivation correction rate. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
