A checklist for brewery taproom event shift handoff tracking should prevent missing decisions, not merely prove that somebody clicked boxes. The checklist below is designed for independent craft breweries operating one or more taprooms and centers on one result: **every taproom event transfers into the operating shift with current commitments, assigned setup, commercial terms, contacts, and explicit manager acceptance**.

## Before the work starts

- Confirm Event client date and agreement version
- Confirm Guest count reserved space and schedule
- Confirm Product service and minimum-spend terms
- Confirm Staff security vendor and performer contacts

Also name the owner and the expected completion condition. If either is unknown, the work is not ready to enter the active queue.

## While the work is moving

- Update Freeze the current event agreement and changes
- Update Translate commitments into shift tasks
- Update Confirm staff vendor space and product readiness
- Update Review and accept at manager handoff
- Update Reconcile event outcome payment and follow-up

Every update should change a decision. Notes such as “followed up” are weak unless they also include the channel, result, next date, and owner.

## Before marking it complete

- Verify Setup equipment power and sound tasks
- Verify Tab deposit payment and closing rules
- Verify Shift manager acceptance and escalation
- Verify Outcome damage cleanup and financial reconciliation

Confirm that the actual outcome—not just an activity—has been recorded. If the process ended early, use a closed reason rather than deleting the record.

## Copy-and-paste weekly review

- [ ] Review records where an event agreement or material change is approved
- [ ] Review records where staff vendor product or space readiness becomes at risk
- [ ] Review records where the event ends with unresolved payment damage or follow-up

- [ ] Check for keeping the latest change only in sales email
- [ ] Check for assigning setup to the shift rather than one owner
- [ ] Check for opening a tab without the agreed closing method
- [ ] Check for closing the event before deposit and damage are reconciled

## Make the checklist measurable

Choose one metric before the next cycle. Good options for this workflow are On-time handoff rate, Day-of surprise rate, Event closeout time. A checklist that never changes a metric or prevents a known failure mode is probably administrative overhead.

## Assign ownership and escalation

Put one role—not a group—next to every item that can remain open. Define a backup owner and an escalation time for work that affects a customer, client, participant, or delivery promise. During review, separate **not started**, **waiting on someone**, and **failed validation**; those states need different actions. If a checklist item repeatedly waits on the same dependency, redesign the intake or handoff instead of adding more reminder boxes.
